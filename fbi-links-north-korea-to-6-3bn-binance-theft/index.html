FBI links North Korea to $6.3bn Binance theft

The FBI says North Korea stole over $6 billion from Binance on Oct. 29, naming TraderTraitor as it urges crypto firms to block the stolen funds.

The Onchain Brief EditorsNov 8, 2026 · 05:45 UTC2 min read

The FBI says North Korea was responsible for stealing more than $6 billion in crypto from Binance on or about Oct. 29. The bureau named the activity “TraderTraitor” and urged crypto firms to block transactions tied to the stolen funds, according to the FBI alert.

Binance put the impact at about $6.34 billion. The exchange said it had frozen or intercepted about $2.85 billion of the assets, subject to changes in value and further checks. It also said it would cover customer losses. As of Oct. 31, Binance reported that 98% of the affected token assets had been replenished.

How did the attackers get access to Binance’s wallets?

Binance says attackers used compromised employee computers and an internal wallet signing service to move funds outside the usual withdrawal process. The company’s initial incident report says the campaign included a fake job interview and a malicious coding assignment that infected an engineer’s work computer.

Binance says the attackers later used an internal AI tool to reach other employees’ computers and credentials. They then used the signing service to authorize transfers from online wallets, which hold crypto for quick access. The company’s investigation is still under way, and its findings are due for independent review.

Where are the stolen funds going?

The FBI says it has seen the attackers convert the stolen assets mainly into Ether, the crypto used on the Ethereum network. It expects them to move the funds through decentralized finance services, or DeFi, which let people trade crypto without a central exchange, and through instant swap services.

The FBI, the U.S. Secret Service and the IRS urged exchanges, blockchain operators and other crypto services to block transactions involving listed wallet addresses. The alert says some addresses are holding or have held assets from the theft. It does not say how much of the stolen crypto has been recovered.

What remains unclear?

The FBI has named North Korea as responsible, but its alert does not describe how investigators reached that conclusion. Binance says its technical account is preliminary and that some details will stay confidential. The full path of the stolen funds and the final amount recovered remain unknown.
