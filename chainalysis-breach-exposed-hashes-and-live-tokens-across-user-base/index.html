Chainalysis breach exposed hashes and live tokens across user base

Chainalysis says names, usernames, password hashes and live tokens were exposed, prompting resets as services return under tighter access controls.

The Onchain Brief EditorsNov 7, 2026 · 23:08 UTC2 min read

Chainalysis said a cyberattack exposed names, usernames, password hashes and live tokens for all its users, putting customer accounts at risk and disrupting services. In its latest security incident update, the blockchain analytics company said it discovered the exposure on Monday and disclosed it late Wednesday, Nov. 4.

The company’s wording does not say whether every listed type of information was exposed for every user. Password hashes are transformed versions of passwords; live tokens can allow access to an account or service. Chainalysis has not explained what kinds of tokens were exposed.

What did Chainalysis say was exposed?

The Nov. 4 update said the exposed information included names, usernames, password hashes and live tokens. Separately, Chainalysis said an “on-path attack”—an attack that intercepts data moving between parties—exposed passwords for a subset of customers. The company said it had contained and fixed that route of attack.

Earlier, on Oct. 30, Chainalysis had reported that user IDs, names, email addresses and organization role labels were accessed. It said then it had found no evidence that attackers had accessed credentials or session tokens. The later disclosures broadened what the company said had been exposed.

Were investigation records accessed?

Chainalysis said it had no evidence that investigation data was accessed or its attribution data manipulated. Investigation data can include graphs, notes or cases used by customers to track crypto activity. The company has not said how many users were affected, how the attacker obtained the data, or who was behind the attack.

Chainalysis also reported abnormal activity in its KYT transaction-monitoring service and said it was notifying customers whose accounts appeared to have been accessed without authorization. It has not published a count of those accounts.

Which services are back online?

As of its Nov. 7 update, Chainalysis said Reactor, KYT and Address Screening were back online with added security measures. It asked customers to follow instructions sent by email to reconnect. The company said users would have to reset passwords and rotate API keys where applicable. Its latest update did not say Data Solutions had returned.

Chainalysis first took Reactor and Data Solutions offline on Nov. 2 while it investigated. It later took KYT, Address Screening products and its login page offline after detecting abnormal activity. The company said it was working with outside security experts, but has not given a timeline for restoring Data Solutions.
