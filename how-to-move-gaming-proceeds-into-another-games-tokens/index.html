How to move gaming proceeds into another game's tokens

Gaming proceeds can fund another game, but only after you match the token, network and fees; this guide explains the swap path and the checks to make first.

The Onchain Brief EditorsOct 16, 2026 · 03:26 UTC3 min read

You can turn gaming proceeds into another game’s tokens by swapping the asset you earned for a token the next game accepts. First find out what you hold: it might be a game token, a stablecoin, or money from selling an in-game item. The route depends on which network the asset and target token use. If you need a step-by-step on quickswapping into an app’s needed asset, that guide covers the swap itself. The checks below help you decide what to swap and where to send it.

What do you need before swapping game proceeds?

Start with the destination game’s own instructions for deposits or purchases. Check its accepted token, network and wallet requirements. A token with the right name on the wrong network may not work. If your proceeds are from an item sale, check whether they arrived as a token or as a balance held inside the game’s marketplace. An in-game balance may need to be withdrawn before you can swap it.

Then check your wallet. It must support the network holding your proceeds and the network used by the target token. You may also need a small amount of that network’s own token to pay transaction fees. A fee is charged for processing the transaction, even when the swap itself is small.

How do you swap proceeds for another game’s token?

Choose a swap service that supports both the asset you have and the token you need. Select the correct network, enter the amount, and review the quote before approving. A quote shows how much of the target token you may receive after fees. “Slippage” means the price can change between the quote and the completed swap, so look for the minimum amount the service says you will receive.

Some swaps happen on one network. If the two tokens live on different networks, you may need a cross-network route, sometimes involving a bridge that moves assets between networks. That can add steps, time and fees. A two-step swap through a widely used asset can be easier to price, but each step may add a fee and the price can move twice.

How do you choose the better route?

Compare the amount you will receive after all costs, not just the displayed exchange rate. For most players, a direct swap on the same network is the simpler choice when it is supported and the quote is reasonable. If you need to compare routes, check:

  • The exact token and network accepted by the destination game.
  • The expected amount received after swap and network fees.
  • Whether you have enough network tokens left to pay fees.
  • Whether the final token can be deposited or used in the game.

Confirm the token address or selection in the wallet when names are similar, and read the transaction details before signing. A swap can complete successfully while still sending funds to an address or token the game does not accept. Keep a record of the transaction if the game requires proof of deposit.

The key is to trace the whole route before moving funds: from the form your proceeds take, through the swap, to the token and network the next game actually supports. Quickswap can describe a fast swap, but speed alone does not make a route suitable. The received amount and compatibility matter more.
