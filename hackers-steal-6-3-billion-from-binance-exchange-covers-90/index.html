Hackers steal $6.3 billion from Binance; exchange covers 90%

Hackers stole about $6.3 billion from Binance in late October; the exchange says it has restored 90% of affected assets and will cover users’ losses.

The Onchain Brief EditorsNov 3, 2026 · 16:00 UTC2 min read

Hackers stole an estimated $6.3 billion in crypto from Binance in late October, turning the world’s largest crypto exchange into a victim of what may be the biggest crypto heist on record, according to The Associated Press’s report on the attack. Binance says it will cover the loss so users do not bear the cost.

The theft is a sharp turn for a company long accused of allowing criminals to use its platform. Binance founder Changpeng Zhao pleaded guilty to violating U.S. rules for preventing money laundering in 2023. He was sentenced to four months in prison in 2024, AP reported. Those past cases concern Binance’s compliance; they do not establish who carried out this attack.

How much was stolen, and who is blamed?

Binance said hackers took about $6.3 billion in bitcoin and other crypto. The FBI attributed the theft to TraderTraitor, a group it links to North Korea, according to AP. North Korea has denied involvement in the heist.

The exchange has not publicly detailed the full attack method. AP reported that the FBI’s attribution followed an assessment by U.S. blockchain intelligence firm TRM, which said the attack was most likely carried out by TraderTraitor. The attribution remains disputed by North Korea.

How much of the loss has Binance covered?

Binance said on October 31 that it had covered 90% of the stolen amount. In its recovery update, the company said it was using its Secure Asset Fund for Users, a reserve for emergencies, part of its futures insurance fund and corporate funds. It said the remaining 10% consisted of less liquid tokens that could take longer to acquire.

Binance said its ten listed major tokens, including bitcoin, ether, BNB and USDT, were fully backed as of that update. It planned to buy the remaining eligible tokens through market trades and direct deals. These are Binance’s statements; the update does not say that the stolen crypto itself has been recovered.

What is still unknown?

Binance has not published a complete account of how the attackers got in or who was responsible. Nor does the 90% coverage figure mean that 90% of stolen coins have been traced or returned. The exchange says it will cover the financial impact on users, but the details of how the attack happened remain under investigation.
