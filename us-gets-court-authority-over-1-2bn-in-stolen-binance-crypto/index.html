US gets court authority over $1.2bn in stolen Binance crypto

The Justice Department says a court has cleared the US to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in crypto stolen from Binance, as tracing continues.

The Onchain Brief EditorsNov 16, 2026 · 10:37 UTC2 min read

The US Justice Department says it has court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance in a theft the FBI links to North Korean hackers. The department announced the action on Nov. 12 in its account of the seizure and related operations.

What the court action covers

The $1.2 billion is part of more than $2.7 billion in stolen funds that US agencies and private companies have frozen, according to the Justice Department. A freeze restricts access to funds; it does not by itself establish that the government has final ownership of them.

The department said it had obtained court authorization to seize or restrain the $1.2 billion. It did not say the full amount had been transferred into government custody. The effort to trace, freeze and seize the assets is continuing, as the attackers move funds through crypto bridges, mixers and exchanges.

FBI links the theft to TraderTraitor

The FBI calls the North Korean military actors behind the theft “TraderTraitor.” The Justice Department said the theft happened on or around Oct. 29 and involved more than $6 billion in virtual assets. The Associated Press reported the FBI’s seizure announcement and said the theft was about $6.3 billion.

Officials said law enforcement worked with virtual asset service providers, companies that handle crypto transactions, to block the stolen funds from moving. The FBI had also issued an alert urging those providers to block transactions involving addresses linked to laundering the Binance assets.

What happens to the funds next

The court authorization is a step toward taking control of the assets, but the final outcome for the money is not yet settled. In similar cases, the government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who decides whether anyone has a legitimate claim to the funds, the Associated Press reported.

The Justice Department has not announced a final forfeiture or return of the Binance funds. It says investigators are still tracing and pursuing them while the actors continue trying to launder the crypto.
