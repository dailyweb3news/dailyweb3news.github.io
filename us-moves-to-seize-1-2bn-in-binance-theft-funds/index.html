US moves to seize $1.2bn in Binance theft funds

The Justice Department says a court authorized action against more than $1.2 billion of crypto stolen from Binance, while efforts to trace and recover funds continue.

The Onchain Brief EditorsNov 15, 2026 · 11:04 UTC2 min read

The US Justice Department has court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, the department said on Nov. 12. The FBI and its partners are still tracing the funds, which have been moved through crypto services to hide their path.

How much of the stolen crypto is frozen?

The Justice Department said government and private-sector efforts have frozen more than $2.7 billion in stolen assets so far. The $1.2 billion is the portion the department has court authorization to seize or restrain; the announcement does not say all of it has been transferred into government custody. The Justice Department’s announcement says the FBI, prosecutors and law-enforcement partners worked with virtual asset service providers, companies that handle crypto transfers, to stop the funds moving beyond their reach.

The FBI said the theft happened on or about Oct. 29 and involved more than $6 billion in virtual assets taken from Binance. The bureau attributes it to North Korean military members it calls “TraderTraitor.” The department says efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the assets are continuing as the actors move funds through crypto bridges, mixers and exchanges.

What happens to the money next?

The court order allows the US to pursue the assets, but it does not settle who will ultimately receive them. In a typical case, the government files a forfeiture claim and a judge decides who has a legitimate claim to the recovered funds, the Associated Press reported. The Justice Department has not said when that process will happen or how much of the $1.2 billion has been recovered in a form that could be returned.

The announcement also covered a separate North Korean-linked operation called FaceStealer. The FBI says it stole more than $400 million in cryptocurrency over 18 months using social engineering, a form of deception designed to trick people into giving access. That figure is separate from the Binance theft and the funds targeted by the seizure authorization.
