Exchange deposit memos need a clear path across chains

An exchange memo can route a deposit to the right account, but a bridge may not carry it across chains; check the final network, address and tag before sending.

The Onchain Brief EditorsOct 16, 2026 · 05:45 UTC3 min read

Exchange deposit memos help credit a transfer to the right account, but they do not automatically travel with funds across chains. A memo, also called a destination tag, is an extra piece of transaction data. Some exchanges use it to tell customers apart when they share one deposit address. A bridge moves assets between networks, and its transfer instructions may be separate from the exchange’s memo field. For a fuller account of how cross-chain routes work, read mayan bridge. The key detail here is whether the exchange’s required deposit information reaches the final transfer.

What does an exchange deposit memo do?

A memo tells the receiving service which account should get credit for a deposit. The address identifies where the transaction lands on the network; the memo can identify the customer within the exchange’s system. The exact setup depends on the asset and exchange. Some deposits need a tag or memo, while others use a unique address and need no extra field.

This differs from a bridge’s own instructions. A bridge may use transaction data to identify the route, asset, or destination wallet. That data is not necessarily the same as the tag an exchange asks you to include. Treat them as separate fields unless the service clearly says otherwise.

What can go wrong when a bridge sends to an exchange?

A transfer can arrive at the exchange’s deposit address but remain unmatched if the required memo is missing or wrong. The bridge may also send a token on a network the exchange does not support for that deposit. In either case, the on-chain transfer can succeed while the exchange account does not show the funds.

Before sending, check these details on the exchange’s deposit screen and in the bridge’s final transfer details:

  • The exact token and network the exchange accepts.
  • The deposit address for that token and network.
  • Whether a memo or tag is required, and its exact value.
  • Whether the bridge lets you enter that memo for the destination transfer.

How can you make a cross-chain deposit more reliable?

Use a direct bridge-to-exchange transfer only when the route clearly supports the exchange’s destination network and memo requirements. Check the final quote or confirmation screen, not just the first address shown. If the bridge offers no place to enter a required memo, do not assume it will add one for you.

For many users, the clearer route is to bridge to a wallet they control, then make a separate deposit to the exchange. This adds a transaction and network fee, but lets you use the exchange’s current address and memo in a normal send. A small test transfer can help confirm the details before moving a larger amount. Keep the transaction ID in case the exchange needs it to investigate a deposit.

The practical rule is simple: confirm the destination chain, address, and memo as one set of instructions. A correct address alone may not be enough, and a memo on the source transfer does not prove the exchange will receive one on the destination side.
