How to manually claim a bridged transfer on Ethereum after a delay

How to manually claim a bridged transfer on Ethereum: wait for signed or approved status, then submit the redeem transaction yourself and pay Ethereum gas.

The Onchain Brief EditorsNov 20, 2026 · 22:34 UTC3 min read

A bridged transfer is claimed manually on Ethereum by submitting its approved message through the bridge’s redeem page or protocol explorer. The Monad bridge route may need this step when the source transaction is confirmed but the destination transfer has not arrived.

Submit the message after it is approved

For a Monad-to-Ethereum transfer, you need the Monad source transaction hash and ETH in your Ethereum wallet for gas. Look up the hash on the explorer for the route your transfer used, then redeem only after its message is signed or approved.

  1. Look up the source transaction. Paste the Monad transaction hash into Wormholescan for a Wormhole route, or Axelarscan for an Axelar route. The explorer shows where the message stands.
  2. Wait for signed or approved status. A Wormhole transfer needs a Guardian-signed VAA, a message that carries the approval for redemption. An Axelar General Message Passing (GMP) call must show approved before manual execution. Before that status appears, there is nothing to submit.

    For NTT routes, a rate-limited transfer sits in the inbound queue and completes after the delay. Wait for the route to make it available before trying to redeem.

  3. Open the redeem or resume page. Enter the source hash, connect your wallet, and switch it to Ethereum. Review and sign the redeem transaction. Keep ETH in that wallet to pay Ethereum gas.

    A standard Wormhole transfer is permissionless: any wallet can submit the VAA. The tokens still go to the recipient encoded in the message, so the submitting wallet does not change who receives them.

  4. Confirm the result. Open the redeem transaction on Etherscan. When it succeeds, check that the recipient’s Ethereum token balance has risen.

Say the Monad deposit is confirmed while the Ethereum balance is unchanged. Before redemption, the source transaction exists but the destination credit is pending. After a successful redeem, the destination transaction is visible and the tokens are credited to the same encoded recipient; the source transfer remains unchanged. The redeem transaction is the step that completes delivery on Ethereum.

The Monad bridge is a concrete example of this flow because its resume or redeem option lets you continue a transfer using the source transaction hash. Select the Ethereum network and sign only once the route’s explorer shows the message is ready.

What does a 'transfer already completed' error mean when redeeming?

This error means the route reports the transfer as completed. Look up the source hash and check for a successful redeem transaction on Etherscan, then check the recipient’s token balance before trying again.

How much ETH does a manual claim need?

A manual claim needs enough ETH in the Ethereum wallet to cover the redeem transaction’s gas. The amount varies with the transaction, so review the wallet’s gas estimate before signing.
