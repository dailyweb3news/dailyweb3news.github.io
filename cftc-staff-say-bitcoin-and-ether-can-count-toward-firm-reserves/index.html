CFTC staff say bitcoin and ether can count toward firm reserves

CFTC staff say derivatives firms may count bitcoin, ether and some stablecoins toward a year of operating resources, with discounts and cash limits.

The Onchain Brief EditorsNov 25, 2026 · 15:14 UTC2 min read

Staff at the Commodity Futures Trading Commission said bitcoin, ether and some payment stablecoins may count toward certain derivatives firms’ financial resources. In a Nov. 23 advisory from the CFTC’s market oversight and clearing divisions, staff described how firms can use the assets to meet rules requiring resources for at least a year of operating costs. The Block reported the same day that the crypto must be discounted in value.

The advisory covers swap execution facilities, designated contract markets and derivatives clearing organizations. These are venues for trading swaps or futures, and organizations that handle the clearing of derivatives trades. The year-long resource requirement is meant to help them keep operating and meet their responsibilities. For readers, the guidance means eligible crypto could help these firms meet part of that requirement.

The amount that counts depends on the asset. Staff said they would not object to a minimum 20% haircut for bitcoin and ether. A haircut is a reduction in the value counted to account for risk. So $100 of either asset could count as $80 under that approach. For payment stablecoins, staff cited a 2% haircut, which would let $98 of every $100 count.

There is also a cash and liquidity limit. A swap execution facility must keep at least three months of operating costs in unencumbered liquid assets, such as cash or highly liquid securities. A designated contract market and a clearing organization must each hold at least six months’ costs in those assets. Crypto, including payment stablecoins, does not qualify as cash or highly liquid securities under the rules cited in the advisory. Those firms therefore cannot use crypto to meet that part of the requirement.

The guidance does not mean every token qualifies. Staff said other crypto assets should be discussed with the relevant CFTC division, and an asset that is not readily marketable and is not a payment stablecoin would generally receive a 100% haircut. The divisions also said firms should consider the risk that business losses and falling crypto prices could occur together.

The advisory gives staff’s view of how existing rules apply. It does not create new binding rules or legal rights, and it is not necessarily the view of the full commission. The letter addresses resources for operating costs; it does not address whether crypto can meet a clearing organization’s separate default-resource requirements.
