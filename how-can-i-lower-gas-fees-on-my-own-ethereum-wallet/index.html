How can I lower gas fees on my own Ethereum wallet?

How to reduce gas fees on your own Ethereum wallet: time the base fee, set a modest tip, use an L2 for routine activity and avoid failed transactions.

The Onchain Brief EditorsNov 21, 2026 · 11:03 UTC3 min read

You can lower fees on your own Ethereum wallet by waiting for a lower base fee, setting a modest tip, using a layer 2 for routine activity and avoiding wasted transactions; urgent sends may still cost more.

When you used an exchange, it handled the transaction settings. With your own wallet, you choose the timing, fee and network. Under EIP-1559, the fee is the base fee plus the priority fee, multiplied by the gas used. The base fee changes with network demand; the priority fee is your tip to validators.

Time the base fee

Ethereum transactions usually cost less when the base fee is lower, so check Etherscan Gas Tracker before sending something that can wait. Send when the tracker shows a quieter period. The base fee can change before your transaction is included, so treat the tracker as a snapshot, not a fixed quote.

Set your own tip

A modest priority tip suits a transaction with no deadline; a higher tip can help when you need it included sooner. Your wallet may also show a max fee. That is a ceiling, not the amount you automatically pay: any unused amount above the base fee and tip is not charged. Raising the ceiling alone costs nothing extra, while lowering the tip can cut the fee when there is no rush.

Use an L2

An L2, or layer 2, suits routine swaps and transfers that do not need to happen on Ethereum mainnet. Networks such as Arbitrum and Base generally have far lower transaction fees. Choose the network before signing and make sure the app and recipient support it.

Avoid failed transactions

A failed transaction still costs gas because the network processed it before it reverted. Before signing, check that you have enough ETH for the fee, that the selected network is right and that your swap’s slippage setting—the price change you will accept—fits the trade. A careful review can prevent paying for an attempt that does not complete.

Batch and schedule housekeeping

Group your approval review into one session and revoke only permissions you no longer need, ideally when fees are low. Each revoke is a separate transaction and costs gas. Approval tools such as revokecash let you inspect and manage token approvals your wallet has granted to smart contracts.

Why are gas fees so different between Ethereum and Base?

Ethereum and Base are separate networks with different transaction costs. Base is an L2, so routine activity there can cost far less than on Ethereum mainnet. The fee you see also depends on network demand and the transaction’s gas use.

What is a gas limit and should I change it?

The gas limit is the maximum gas a transaction can use. It is not the same as the fee: the fee depends on gas actually used and the per-unit price. Keep the wallet’s suggested limit unless you understand why it needs changing.

Stuck transactions and L2 fees

Can I speed up or cancel a stuck transaction?

Many wallets let you replace a pending transaction with one using a higher priority fee, or send a replacement that cancels it. The replacement must use the same account and nonce. Check your wallet’s options; a replacement can also cost gas.

Do I need ETH on each layer 2 to pay fees?

Usually, yes: you need the network’s accepted gas token on that specific L2. ETH on Ethereum mainnet is separate from ETH on Base or Arbitrum, so make sure funds are on the network you plan to use.
