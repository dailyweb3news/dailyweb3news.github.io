US wins court approval to seize $1.2B in Binance hack funds

The Justice Department says it won court approval to seize or restrain $1.2 billion tied to Binance’s $6.3 billion hack, as efforts to trace the funds continue.

The Onchain Brief EditorsNov 22, 2026 · 03:14 UTC2 min read

The Justice Department says it has court approval to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance in a hack last month. The FBI attributes the theft to North Korean hackers known as TraderTraitor, according to the Justice Department’s announcement on Nov. 12.

What the $1.2 billion action covers

The authorized amount is part of more than $2.7 billion in stolen assets frozen so far by the U.S. government and private-sector partners, the department said. The theft, which happened on or around Oct. 29, involved more than $6 billion in virtual assets.

That distinction matters: the department says it has obtained court authorization to seize or restrain the $1.2 billion. It does not say that the entire amount has been recovered or returned to Binance or its users. The freeze total also includes assets that the department has not said are covered by this court authorization.

How investigators are tracking the funds

U.S. agencies have worked with cryptocurrency service providers to block movement of the stolen assets. The Justice Department says the hackers continue to launder funds through crypto bridges, mixers and exchanges. Bridges move assets between blockchains, while mixers obscure where funds came from.

The FBI has identified TraderTraitor as the group behind the theft. The department describes its members as part of North Korea’s military. The FBI and Justice Department say efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the assets are continuing.

What happens to the seized crypto

Seizure or restraint does not settle who will ultimately receive the funds. In cases like this, the U.S. government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who decides who has a legitimate claim, the Associated Press reported.

The department has not said when that process will be completed or how much of the stolen crypto may be returned. For now, the $1.2 billion figure describes assets covered by court authorization, while the wider effort to track and block the hackers’ transfers remains in progress.
