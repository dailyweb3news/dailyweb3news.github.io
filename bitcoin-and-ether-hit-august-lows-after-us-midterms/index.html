Bitcoin and ether hit August lows after US midterms

Bitcoin and ether fell to their lowest prices since August as traders weighed a Democratic-controlled Congress and recent crypto shocks, leaving the market under pressure.

The Onchain Brief EditorsNov 10, 2026 · 01:29 UTC2 min read

Bitcoin and ether fell to their lowest prices since August in late Sunday trading, as investors worried a Democratic-controlled US Congress could bring tougher crypto rules. Reuters reported bitcoin fell more than 2% to $73,708 and ether about 5% to $2,223.

How far did bitcoin and ether fall?

Both prices touched lows not seen for more than two months. Reuters said the fall followed the election of prominent crypto critics in last week’s midterms. Democrats won control of Congress, and investors are weighing what that could mean for rules governing digital assets. The report did not identify specific legislation or say what changes lawmakers will pursue.

The drop came after a sharp reversal earlier in the week. The Block reported that bitcoin had climbed back above $80,000 on Wednesday, then fell 6% to $75,700 on Thursday. Ether dropped more than 7% to $2,247 that day, as a selloff in US technology stocks spread into crypto markets.

What else has weighed on the market?

Reuters said crypto markets had also been shaken by a $6 billion heist at Binance, the world’s largest crypto exchange. Its report linked the recent pressure to both the heist and the midterm results; it did not say either alone caused Sunday’s fall.

The Block reported that Thursday’s market drop triggered nearly $1.1 billion in forced liquidations across crypto derivatives, affecting about 211,000 traders. A liquidation closes a trader’s position when losses or collateral rules require it. That figure describes Thursday’s selloff, not the late Sunday move.

What remains uncertain about US crypto rules?

The election result has raised concern about the direction of policy, but the reports do not establish what the new Congress will do. Reuters described investor worries that lawmakers could toughen regulation. The Block reported that analysts expected bipartisan support would be needed for major crypto legislation, and said the outlook for the proposed Clarity Act was uncertain.

For now, the confirmed development is the price drop: bitcoin and ether reached their lowest levels since August. Whether that marks a longer downturn, or how US policy will change, remains unclear.
