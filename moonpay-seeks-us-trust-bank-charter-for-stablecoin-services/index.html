MoonPay seeks US trust bank charter for stablecoin services

MoonPay has applied for a US trust bank charter to offer custody and stablecoin services under federal oversight, but the OCC has yet to decide.

The Onchain Brief EditorsNov 23, 2026 · 20:59 UTC2 min read

MoonPay has applied to the US Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) to set up a national trust bank for custody and stablecoin services. The filing proposes a bank that would not take deposits. The OCC has not yet decided whether to approve it, according to MoonPay’s public charter application.

The application is dated November 17, 2026, and the OCC received it on November 18. It names the proposed institution MoonPay National Trust Bank and gives its main office address as Miami Beach, Florida. MoonPay Inc would own the bank.

MoonPay says the proposed bank would provide fiduciary custody, which means holding assets on behalf of clients under a duty to protect their interests. It also lists stablecoin issuance, redemption and reserve management, along with currency conversion as an agent. Stablecoins are digital tokens designed to track the value of an asset such as the US dollar.

What the proposed charter would cover

The application describes the bank as a non-depository national bank, with operations limited to trust company services and related activities. It says the bank would not seek federal deposit insurance and would apply for Federal Reserve membership. These are plans in the filing; they do not mean the OCC has approved the bank or its services.

MoonPay’s application says it wants to offer the services through one national trust bank. The Block reported that the charter, if approved, would provide a single federal framework for the company’s custody and stablecoin services. MoonPay already has a New York trust charter, granted in November 2025, and received a New York BitLicense in June that year, according to The Block’s report.

The OCC’s decision is still ahead

The next step is regulatory review. The public filing does not give a decision date, and the OCC has not announced an approval. MoonPay’s proposed bank therefore cannot yet be described as operating under the requested national charter.

The application also leaves key parts of the proposal out of the public record. MoonPay submitted its business plan, financial projections and permissibility analysis as confidential exhibits. The public filing confirms the services it wants to provide, but it does not show the full plan or how the OCC will assess it.
