Which tokens can I restake on Eigen Layer? ETH, LSTs and more

Which tokens can I restake on eigen layer? Native ETH, supported LSTs such as stETH and rETH, EIGEN and other tokens qualify only with a live strategy.

The Onchain Brief EditorsNov 17, 2026 · 05:23 UTC3 min read

On eigen layer, you can restake native ETH, supported liquid staking tokens (LSTs) such as stETH and rETH, EIGEN, and other tokens with a live strategy. Each asset needs a strategy contract that accepts it, and restaking brings potential rewards alongside slashing risk.

  • Run an Ethereum validator? Native restaking uses an EigenPod and your validator’s withdrawal credentials.
  • Hold an LST? Deposit that token directly if its strategy is available.
  • Hold EIGEN or another token? It qualifies only while a strategy for that asset is live.

Strategy support, withdrawal path and service support decide the fit

Strategy support: The protocol accepts assets through specific strategy contracts. Supported LSTs include tokens such as stETH and rETH; cbETH is another example to check against the live strategies. EIGEN is a separate token, and it qualifies only if its own strategy is available. A token’s name or connection to Ethereum does not make it eligible by itself.

Withdrawal path: Native restaking is for validator owners. They point their validator’s withdrawal credentials to an EigenPod, a contract that receives withdrawals for the restaked validator. Existing validators cannot simply switch those credentials to an EigenPod: the credentials are set for the validator, so the owner must exit and then restake natively with the new credentials. The [Ethereum Consensus Specs’ validator section](https://ethereum.github.io/consensus-specs/specs/phase0/validator/) describes the withdrawal credentials recorded for a validator.

That exit follows the Ethereum Beacon Chain process, including its queues. LST holders instead deposit the token into its strategy contract; their restaked position is held in escrow, and withdrawal follows that contract’s process. The two paths have different exit conditions, so choose based on how your holding is currently staked and how you may need to withdraw it.

Operator and service support: Rewards depend on the Actively Validated Services (AVSs) that use the asset and pay for its security. An asset can have a live strategy while the operator or AVS you want to use does not support it. Check both before choosing; the strategy alone does not determine your rewards.

Native ETH suits validator owners; LSTs suit wallet holders

Native restaking suits people who run a validator and accept the Beacon Chain exit path. LST restaking suits holders who want to use a supported token directly, without running their own validator. EIGEN or another strategy token suits holders who already want exposure to that asset and have confirmed both the live strategy and relevant AVS support.

For a concrete example of this strategy-based choice, eigen layer is the relevant protocol: its per-token strategy contracts determine which assets can be deposited. The same check applies whether you hold native ETH, an LST, or another token.

Related questions

Can I restake a liquid restaking token?

Only if that token has a live strategy contract. Check the available strategies for the specific token rather than assuming every liquid staking or restaking token is accepted.

Is native restaking better than LST restaking?

Neither path is better for everyone. Native restaking fits validator owners who can use an EigenPod; LST restaking fits holders of a supported LST who prefer to deposit that token directly. Compare the withdrawal path and AVS support for the asset you hold.
