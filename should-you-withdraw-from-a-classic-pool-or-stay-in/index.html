Should You Withdraw From a Classic Pool or Stay In?

Classic Pool LPs should stay only if swap fees justify the changing token mix and they still want exposure; otherwise, withdrawing resets the decision.

The Onchain Brief EditorsNov 4, 2026 · 05:42 UTC3 min read

Classic Pool LPs should stay only if they still want both tokens and the fees make the risk worthwhile. A liquidity provider deposits two tokens so traders can swap between them. In return, the provider earns a share of trading fees, but the value and mix of the tokens can change.

What happens when you withdraw from a Classic Pool?

Withdrawing turns your share of the pool back into the two tokens at their current amounts. A Classic Pool follows a constant-product formula: as traders buy one token, its supply in the pool falls while the other token’s supply rises. Your share reflects that changed mix, plus any fees retained by the pool.

That means you may not get back the same quantities you deposited. If one token rose in value against the other, the pool usually holds less of the rising token and more of the falling one than a simple hold would. This difference is called impermanent loss; it becomes a real result when you withdraw. For a walkthrough of the setup, see SyncSwap’s token conversion and supply steps. The same basic pool mechanics help explain why a withdrawal returns a changed mix.

When does it make sense to stay in a Classic Pool?

Staying can make sense when you would be comfortable holding either token, expect trading activity to continue, and accept that fees may not cover price moves. Fees depend on swaps and pool rules; they are not a fixed payment. A displayed yield is a snapshot, not a promise that the return will continue.

Think through three questions before leaving a position in place:

  • Would you still choose to hold these two tokens today?
  • Are fees coming in at a pace that makes the exposure worthwhile?
  • Could you accept a larger share of the weaker-performing token after a price move?

If the answers point to staying, holding the position avoids making a rushed decision based on one short-term price change. Check the pool’s current fee and token balances, and remember that fees can vary with trading activity.

When should you withdraw from a Classic Pool?

Withdraw when you need the funds, no longer want exposure to either token, or judge that the likely fees do not compensate for the risk. You do not need to wait for a particular price level. A partial withdrawal can reduce the position while leaving some liquidity in the pool, if the interface allows it.

Before confirming, check the amounts and network shown in the transaction. If your LP share is deposited in a separate farm or rewards program, you may need to remove it there first. Once the tokens are back in your wallet, you can decide whether to hold them, sell one, or supply them again. Treat that as a fresh choice: past fees do not make a pool a better fit today.
