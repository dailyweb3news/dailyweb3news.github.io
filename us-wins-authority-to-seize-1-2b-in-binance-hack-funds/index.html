US wins authority to seize $1.2B in Binance hack funds

US authorities say they have frozen $2.7 billion from the Binance theft and won court authority to seize or restrain $1.2 billion as recovery efforts continue.

The Onchain Brief EditorsNov 19, 2026 · 09:10 UTC2 min read

US authorities have won court approval to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, the Justice Department said on Thursday, November 12. The funds are part of more than $2.7 billion frozen since the theft, according to the department’s announcement.

What the court order covers

The $1.2 billion is part of the larger amount frozen through work by US authorities and private companies. The Justice Department said it had obtained court authorization to seize or restrain that portion. The order does not mean the government has already returned the cryptocurrency to Binance or its customers.

The Associated Press reported that the FBI said more than $1.2 billion had been seized. It also noted that the Justice Department described the court action as authority to seize or restrain funds. In such cases, the government typically files a forfeiture claim, and a judge decides who has a legitimate claim to the recovered assets.

How much was stolen, and who is blamed

The Justice Department said the theft happened on or about October 29 and involved more than $6 billion in virtual assets. The FBI calls the North Korean military-linked actors behind it “TraderTraitor.” The government’s statement did not name individual suspects.

Since the theft, the combined effort by government agencies and private firms has frozen more than $2.7 billion, the department said. That is a portion of the more than $6 billion stolen. The US has not said how much of the frozen crypto can ultimately be recovered or when any funds might be returned.

Why recovery is still under way

Authorities say the hackers continue to move stolen assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. Bridges move crypto between different blockchains; mixers can make a transaction trail harder to follow. The FBI and its partners are working to trace and freeze the funds, while seeking seizure and forfeiture orders.

The court action is one step in that process. The Justice Department said efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the assets are ongoing. Its announcement did not provide a timeline for resolving claims or distributing any recovered cryptocurrency.
