Fairshake plans $1M pushes in six House races

Fairshake named 32 House incumbents for November support, with $1 million in planned spending for six, as the Senate’s crypto market rules bill remains stalled.

The Onchain Brief EditorsOct 8, 2026 · 17:53 UTC2 min read

Fairshake plans to spend at least $6 million supporting six House candidates from both parties, after the Senate blocked a major crypto market rules bill. The group’s full slate covers 32 incumbents: 19 Republicans and 13 Democrats, according to The Associated Press’s report on Fairshake’s announcement.

Who gets the six $1 million spending plans?

Fairshake plans $1 million in spending for three Democrats: Reps. Janelle Bynum of Oregon, Steven Horsford of Nevada and Derek Tran of California. The three Republicans are Reps. French Hill of Arkansas, Bryan Steil of Wisconsin and Bill Huizenga of Michigan.

The plans are for outside spending, such as campaign ads, rather than $1 million checks to the candidates. Fairshake has not disclosed how much it plans to spend on the other 26 House races. The group and its affiliated PACs reported about $120 million in cash at the end of August, AP reported.

What connects the 32 lawmakers?

All 32 backed legislation that was a top industry priority. The House passed the CLARITY Act in July 2025. The bill aimed to set rules for digital asset markets and divide oversight between federal regulators.

The measure stalled in the Senate for more than a year. In September, senators blocked it in a procedural vote after failing to agree on ethics rules. Critics said the bill did not adequately address President Donald Trump’s crypto investments, according to AP. The industry wanted the bill to give crypto firms a clearer federal regulatory framework.

Will Fairshake spend more before November?

That remains unknown. Fairshake has not said whether it will put more money into House or Senate races before the midterms. The group’s slate is part of a broader effort to support candidates it considers pro-crypto as the industry looks to the next Congress to revive stalled legislation.

Fairshake also announced plans in September to spend nearly $30 million opposing Democratic Senate candidate Sherrod Brown in Ohio. Brown is running against Republican Sen. Jon Husted. The House spending plan adds support for incumbents in both parties, but the amounts for most of those races are still undisclosed, Roll Call reported.
