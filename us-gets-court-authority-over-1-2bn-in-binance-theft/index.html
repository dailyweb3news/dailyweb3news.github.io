US gets court authority over $1.2bn in Binance theft

US officials secured court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2bn of crypto stolen from Binance, part of a wider effort to freeze the stolen funds.

The Onchain Brief EditorsNov 18, 2026 · 10:44 UTC2 min read

US officials have court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, the Justice Department said on November 12. The funds are part of a larger pool frozen after the theft, and efforts to recover them are continuing, according to the department’s announcement.

More than $2.7 billion has been frozen

The Justice Department said the theft happened on or about October 29. It said members of North Korea’s military, whom the FBI calls TraderTraitor, stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from the exchange.

Since then, US agencies and private companies have worked with crypto service providers to block the stolen funds from moving. More than $2.7 billion has been frozen so far, the department said. The court-authorized amount of more than $1.2 billion is part of that total, not an additional sum.

A freeze can stop crypto from being transferred while officials seek legal control of it. The department said its authority covers seizure or restraint. It did not say the funds have been returned to Binance or its users.

The recovery process is still underway

The stolen assets are still being traced, frozen and pursued for forfeiture, the Justice Department said. Forfeiture is the legal process used to transfer property to the government. The department said the attackers continue to move assets through crypto bridges, mixers and exchanges. These services can move or obscure the trail of funds between wallets.

The Associated Press reported on the FBI announcement and noted that a judge typically decides who has a legitimate claim to recovered assets in forfeiture cases. That means court authority to seize funds does not by itself settle who will ultimately receive them.

Officials attribute the theft to TraderTraitor

The FBI has attributed the Binance theft to TraderTraitor, a name it uses for North Korean military hackers, according to the Justice Department. The department said the government and private sector moved quickly to freeze funds before they could be moved beyond law enforcement’s reach.

The value of crypto can change, and the recovery work is ongoing. The Justice Department’s announcement did not give a breakdown of which coins make up the court-authorized amount or say when the legal process will be complete.
