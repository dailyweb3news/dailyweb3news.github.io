One large buy vs several small buys for a new token: what to weigh

One large buy vs several small buys for a new token: one buy usually saves fees, while a small first buy and test sell can help check the exit.

The Onchain Brief EditorsNov 26, 2026 · 06:11 UTC3 min read

One large buy usually pays less in fees than several back-to-back small buys, while both move a Raydium pool to about the same final price for the same total amount. That is because a standard pool’s price follows the constant-product formula x*y=k: the total amount bought against the reserves drives the price move.

Before buying, run a rugcheck on the mint for warning signs of a scam or rug pull. Use token risk analysis as part of that review, then look at the swap quote and the pool’s depth before deciding how much to enter.

Back-to-back buys cost more fees for nearly the same price move

One large buy gives you one quote showing the full price impact: how much your trade moves the pool price. Several small buys made in a row add up to nearly the same price impact, assuming no other trades change the pool between them. Splitting the order does not make the same total buy cheaper through price impact alone.

Each swap also uses a transaction. On Solana, every transaction pays a base fee of 5,000 lamports per signature, plus any priority fee. So several swaps mean several base fees, and priority fees may add to each transaction too. Slippage is different from price impact: it is the price movement you allow between getting a quote and the swap completing.

Compare the total buy with the liquidity pool’s depth. When the quote shows high price impact, reduce the amount. Slicing the same large buy into pieces still pushes against the same reserves, and the extra swaps add fees.

A small first buy and test sell can check the exit

Several buys can be useful when the token is unproven and you want to check that selling works before committing the rest. Make a small first buy, then sell a tiny amount back into the same pool. A completed test sell confirms that your wallet can exit through that pool at that moment.

That check matters because a buy quote alone only describes the entry. A rugcheck can flag warning signs, but the test sell gives a direct check of the swap path you plan to use. It costs another transaction and may incur price impact, so keep the test small.

Splitting can also change the result if there are gaps between buys and the price recovers during those gaps. That depends on what happens in the pool while you wait; it is not a fee saving you can count on from splitting alone.

Choose one buy when the quoted price impact is small and the token sells normally. It keeps transaction fees down. Choose a small first buy, then test-sell before buying the rest when the token is unproven and checking the exit is worth the added swap fees. If the quote’s price impact is high, buy less rather than splitting the same amount.
