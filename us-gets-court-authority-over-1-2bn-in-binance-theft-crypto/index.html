US gets court authority over $1.2bn in Binance theft crypto

US authorities say they froze more than $2.7 billion tied to the Binance theft, with court approval to seize or restrain over $1.2 billion of it.

The Onchain Brief EditorsNov 13, 2026 · 11:55 UTC2 min read

US authorities say they have frozen more than $2.7 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, and the Justice Department has court approval to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of it. The Associated Press report says the FBI announced the action on Thursday. The Justice Department’s announcement says efforts to trace and recover the funds are continuing.

What has the US secured?

The $1.2 billion figure is part of the more than $2.7 billion that government agencies and private companies have frozen since the theft, according to the Justice Department. A freeze blocks funds from being moved. Court authorization to seize or restrain assets gives the government legal authority to pursue them, but does not by itself settle who will ultimately receive them.

The department said it worked with virtual asset service providers, companies that handle cryptocurrency, to stop the stolen funds from moving beyond law enforcement’s reach. It did not identify the providers or give a breakdown of the frozen amount.

How much was stolen, and who does the FBI blame?

The Justice Department says more than $6 billion in virtual assets was stolen from Binance on or about Oct. 29. It attributes the theft to members of the North Korean military whom the FBI calls TraderTraitor. The AP describes the loss as about $6.3 billion and reports that the FBI had attributed it to the North Korean hacking group.

The Justice Department says the attackers continue to launder assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. Bridges move assets between blockchains; mixers are services designed to make a transaction trail harder to follow. The department says work to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the funds is ongoing.

What happens to the recovered funds?

The latest announcement does not say that the full $1.2 billion has been returned to Binance or to affected users. The AP says the US government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then decides who has a valid claim to recovered funds. The Justice Department’s release does not set out a timetable for that process or say how much of the frozen cryptocurrency has already been transferred into government custody.
