US gets court approval to seize $1.2bn from Binance hack

The Justice Department won court approval to seize or restrain $1.2 billion from the Binance hack, as investigators continue tracing the stolen funds.

The Onchain Brief EditorsNov 14, 2026 · 13:46 UTC2 min read

The US Justice Department has court approval to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, the department said on November 12. The FBI and other agencies are still tracing the funds. The Justice Department’s announcement says the theft happened on or about October 29.

The FBI attributes the theft to North Korean military members it calls TraderTraitor. The department says they stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance. That attribution is the government’s assessment; the announcement does not name suspects or describe any charges tied to this theft.

More than $2.7 billion has been frozen

Government and private-sector efforts have frozen more than $2.7 billion of the stolen assets so far, according to the Justice Department. The $1.2 billion covered by court authorization is part of that frozen amount. The authorization lets the government seek to seize or restrain those assets; it does not mean all the funds have been returned to victims.

The Associated Press report says the government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then decides who has a valid claim to recovered funds. The Justice Department’s announcement does not say when a final decision will be made or how much, if any, will be returned to Binance or affected users.

Investigators are following funds across crypto services

The department says the suspected hackers are continuing to move assets through crypto bridges, mixers and exchanges. Bridges move tokens between blockchains. Mixers are services that can make the trail between crypto transactions harder to follow.

Last week, the FBI, Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation issued an alert that linked the theft to TraderTraitor and urged crypto services to block transactions involving listed addresses, including transactions derived from them. The Justice Department says the FBI’s Los Angeles office, Cyber Division and Virtual Asset Unit are coordinating recovery work with those agencies.

The government’s announcement also describes a separate operation against FaceStealer, a North Korean social engineering scheme that officials say stole more than $400 million in cryptocurrency over 18 months. That figure is not part of the Binance theft or the $1.2 billion covered by the court authorization.
