Slippage Tolerance: Set a Safe Minimum on a DEX

To choose slippage tolerance when a DEX shows high price impact, start with the smallest setting that works; the quote already includes price impact.

The Onchain Brief EditorsNov 24, 2026 · 23:34 UTC3 min read

To choose slippage tolerance when a DEX shows high price impact, use the smallest tolerance that lets the swap proceed. Price impact is already reflected in the quoted output; tolerance sets how much lower your final output may be before the swap reverts.

How do you choose slippage tolerance when price impact is high?

On ShibaSwap, as on other DEXs, first read the quoted output after price impact. Then use the minimum received figure to see the lowest output the swap will accept at your chosen tolerance. Price impact changes the quote; tolerance applies to that quote.

For more on how trade size affects the quote, see ShibaSwap price impact by trade size. That helps explain why a large trade can get a lower quoted output before you set tolerance.

Say an illustrative ShibaSwap quote shows you would receive 98 tokens after price impact. At 1% tolerance, the minimum output is 98 × (1 − 0.01), or 97.02 tokens. The 98-token quote stays the reference point; the tolerance allows the output to fall by up to 0.98 token before the swap reverts.

A higher tolerance makes the swap more likely to execute while the market moves, but it also lowers the minimum output. It does not undo the price impact or improve the quote. Choose a setting by asking whether the resulting minimum is still acceptable to you.

If the minimum is too low, or the swap needs a tolerance that feels high, reduce the trade size, compare available routes, or stop. Changing the size or route may change the quoted output; your tolerance still sets the permitted drop from that new quote. If no acceptable quote gives you a suitable minimum, do not swap.

What does minimum received mean on a DEX?

Minimum received is the lowest output your swap can deliver and still complete at the selected tolerance. With a 98-token quote and 1% tolerance, it is 97.02 tokens. If the output falls below that floor before execution, the swap reverts.

Why can a swap fail with slippage set too low?

A swap can fail when the output changes beyond the permitted amount between the quote and execution. A low tolerance sets a tighter floor, so even a small move can push the expected output below minimum received. The trade may then revert instead of completing.

Read the quoted output, calculate the minimum received, and proceed only if that floor works for you. If it does not, change the size or route, or leave the swap.
