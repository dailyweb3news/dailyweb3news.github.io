Why did sending to a new TRON address cost extra TRX? The one-time account fee

Sending TRX to a new TRON address can cost extra because the first transfer creates its on-chain account, adding a one-time fee and Bandwidth cost.

The Onchain Brief EditorsNov 24, 2026 · 05:05 UTC3 min read

Sending TRX to a new TRON address cost extra TRX because its first incoming TRX transfer creates an on-chain account, adding a governance-set account-creation fee and Bandwidth cost to the ordinary transfer. SunSwap swaps TRC-20 tokens through liquidity pools on TRON, where contract transfers follow different activation rules. TRON’s official account activation rules explain when a transfer creates an account and how the sender pays.

Why does the first transfer cost more?

A wallet can generate an address before TRON has an account record for it. The network creates that record when the address first receives TRX or a TRC-10 token. Creating new on-chain state costs resources, so the first qualifying transfer pays an account-creation fee as well as its normal Bandwidth cost.

Free daily Bandwidth does not cover account creation. The sender’s staked or delegated Bandwidth may cover the transaction’s Bandwidth requirement; otherwise, TRX is burned for that cost. The fee is the governance-set account-creation fee plus any Bandwidth burn. Super Representative governance can change the relevant parameters, so check the current values in TRONSCAN’s chain parameters instead of relying on an old fee estimate.

Say you send TRX to a friend’s fresh TronLink address. You pay the extra charge because your transfer creates the account. A second TRX transfer to that same address does not create it again, so it pays only the ordinary transfer cost. The extra account fee applies once per address, and you can inspect the transaction’s resource details on TRONSCAN to see the burn.

Who pays the activation fee, sender or receiver?

The sender pays the activation fee when their transfer creates the account. The receiver does not need to make a separate payment for that creation. Keep enough TRX available for the first transfer’s fee and any Bandwidth burn, even if your regular transfers often use free daily Bandwidth.

Does sending USDT to a new address also activate it?

No. USDT on TRON is a TRC-20 token, and a TRC-20 transfer alone does not activate a fresh account under TRON’s standard activation rules. Do not assume a token deposit has created the account; check the address on TRONSCAN. A TRX transfer can activate it.

The same distinction matters with SunSwap. Its swaps send TRC-20 tokens through smart contracts, so a token payout to a fresh address should not be treated as the standard TRX account-creation transfer. A swap can have its own resource costs, but that is a separate charge. For a new receiving address, first send TRX if you need to establish its account; that makes the activation step and its cost clear before you rely on the address for later transfers.
