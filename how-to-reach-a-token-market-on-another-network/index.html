How to Reach a Token Market on Another Network

To reach a token market on another blockchain, move funds onto that network first or use a cross-chain swap, then check the token and route before signing.

The Onchain Brief EditorsOct 16, 2026 · 23:20 UTC3 min read

To reach a token market on another network, get funds onto that blockchain and then swap for the token there. A token’s name alone is not enough: the same name can refer to different assets, and a token may exist on one network but not another. First identify the network and token contract address shown by the project or market you intend to use.

There are two common routes. You can bridge an asset you already hold to the destination network, then trade it on a market there. Or you can use a cross-chain swap, which combines moving value between networks and exchanging it for the destination token. For a closer look at how one route is framed, read this Biswap Polygon-to-Bitcoin route explainer. The exact steps and available assets depend on the route.

What do I need before I start?

You need a wallet that can access both networks, the token you will spend, and enough funds for fees. A network fee, often called gas, pays for transactions on a blockchain. You may need some of the destination network’s own coin to trade or move the received token after it arrives. Some routes account for this in their quote; others do not.

Check that the destination network matches the market listing. A token address is a useful identifier on many smart contract networks, but addresses and token formats differ across chains. If you send an asset to a network or address it does not support, recovery may be difficult or impossible.

Should I bridge first or use a cross-chain swap?

Bridge first when you want to move an asset you plan to keep on the new network. The bridge may lock or burn the asset on one chain and release or create a corresponding version on another. You then make a separate trade. This gives you a chance to choose a market after the transfer, but it means more steps and usually more than one transaction.

A cross-chain swap can be simpler when your goal is to receive a particular token on the destination network. It bundles the transfer and trade into one route. The quoted amount can reflect fees, exchange rates and price impact—the effect of your trade size on the price. Routes vary, so compare the final amount and the time estimate before choosing.

What should I check before signing?

Review the route as a transaction plan, not just a token price. Confirm:

  • The source and destination networks are correct.
  • The token you will receive is the one identified by the project or market.
  • The estimated amount after fees and the minimum amount the route promises to deliver.
  • Whether you will have destination-network funds left for later fees.

Then check what your wallet is asking you to approve. A token approval gives a contract permission to use a specified token amount; it is separate from the swap itself. Sign only the transactions you understand, and keep the transaction record so you can check its status. If the route fails or stalls, the result depends on the bridge or swap service and the transaction stage; a pending transfer does not always mean funds are lost.

The practical choice is simple: use a bridge when you want to move an asset, and consider a cross-chain swap when you want a specific token on the other network. In either case, confirm the network and token identity before signing.
