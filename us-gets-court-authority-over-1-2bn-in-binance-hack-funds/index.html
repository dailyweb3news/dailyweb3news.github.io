US gets court authority over $1.2bn in Binance hack funds

US authorities have court approval to seize or restrain more than $1.2bn from Binance’s $6.3bn hack, while efforts to trace and recover the rest continue.

The Onchain Brief EditorsNov 15, 2026 · 05:34 UTC2 min read

US authorities have court approval to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, the Justice Department said Thursday. The move covers part of a wider effort to freeze assets linked to the theft, which the FBI attributes to North Korean hackers.

The Justice Department said in its Nov. 12 announcement that the theft happened on or about Oct. 29. It said the North Korean military group the FBI calls “TraderTraitor” stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance. The Associated Press reported the theft at about $6.3 billion.

More than $2.7bn has been frozen

The $1.2 billion is part of a larger pool of funds frozen since the theft. The Justice Department said government agencies and private companies have so far frozen more than $2.7 billion in stolen assets. It has obtained court approval to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of that amount.

That distinction matters: the announcement describes court authority to seize or restrain the funds, while the wider total refers to assets frozen. US officials and private firms worked with virtual asset service providers, or companies that handle crypto transfers and storage, to stop funds moving beyond their reach.

Tracing and recovery are still underway

The government has not said it has recovered all the assets covered by the court order. The Justice Department said efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the stolen cryptocurrency are ongoing. It said TraderTraitor-linked actors continue to move assets through crypto bridges, mixers and exchanges. Bridges move assets between blockchains; mixers can make their transaction paths harder to trace.

The AP reported that the US government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge in these cases. The judge then decides who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds. The announcement did not say when such a claim would be filed or how much of the frozen cryptocurrency will ultimately be returned.

The FBI links the theft to North Korea

The FBI had previously attributed the Binance theft to TraderTraitor, a name it uses for North Korean military cyber actors. The Justice Department said US agencies are working with private companies to track and block the stolen assets. The FBI’s recovery work is being coordinated by its Los Angeles field office, Cyber Division and Virtual Asset Unit, alongside the US Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation.
