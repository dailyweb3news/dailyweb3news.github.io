Bitcoin falls below $82,000 as liquidations hit $771 million

Bitcoin fell 2.8% to $81,382 on Monday as $771 million in crypto positions were forcibly closed in 24 hours, most of them bets on rising prices.

The Onchain Brief EditorsOct 13, 2026 · 10:32 UTC2 min read

Bitcoin fell below $82,000 on Monday evening as crypto traders lost $771 million in positions that were forcibly closed over 24 hours, according to The Block’s market report. The forced closures matter because they can add to selling when prices fall.

Bitcoin was at $81,382 at 9:20 p.m. Eastern time, down 2.8% over 24 hours and from about $84,200 earlier that day. Ether fell 4.6% to $2,425. Among the 10 largest cryptocurrencies, Zcash dropped the most, falling 10.4% to $1,149. Solana lost 7.5%, and XRP fell 6.4%.

How much of the $771 million came from bets on rising prices?

About $685.6 million came from long positions, bets that prices would rise, CoinGlass data cited by The Block showed. In the 12 hours before the report, liquidations totalled $631.8 million, including $600 million in long positions.

A liquidation happens when an exchange forcibly closes a trader’s position after losses leave too little margin to keep it open. When many long positions are closed during a price drop, those sales can add to downward pressure. CoinGlass gathers data from public channels and exchange APIs; it says its figures may not capture every liquidation.

What did the report identify as pressure on crypto prices?

The Block quoted Zeus Research analyst Dominick John, who pointed to high Treasury yields, a stronger U.S. dollar and positioning ahead of U.S. inflation data as possible headwinds. John also cited about $600 million in outflows from spot Bitcoin exchange-traded funds, investment products that hold Bitcoin.

The report said those funds recorded $681.1 million in net outflows last week, their worst week since late June, citing SoSoValue data. Spot Ether funds had $542 million in net outflows over the same week.

The report also noted a sharp fall in South Korean shares on Tuesday morning in Asia. The Kospi was down 6.1% at 10:45 a.m. local time. Bitmine chairman Tom Lee said the decline could be spilling over into crypto. The report did not establish how much any one factor contributed to Bitcoin’s fall.
