What Do You Need to Bridge Crypto From a Self-Custody Wallet?

What do you need to bridge crypto from a self-custody wallet? A supported route, the asset and gas on its source network, and a destination address.

The Onchain Brief EditorsNov 24, 2026 · 02:33 UTC3 min read

To bridge crypto from a self-custody wallet, you need the asset on a supported source network, a route that supports that asset and your destination network, enough gas, and a destination address. The bridge uses on-chain transactions, so the networks and asset must match the route, and you must authorize the transfer with your wallet.

Keep the recovery phrase private, and use a wallet you control. Wallet signatures authorize transactions; a bridge cannot move tokens from your wallet without the required authorization. Ethereum, Arbitrum, Optimism, BNB Chain, and Polygon are separate networks, each with its own native gas token.

That signature requirement is one reason a bridge transfer differs from moving funds inside an exchange. cBridge is one example of an on-chain route; its wallet signatures make it useful for understanding what changes in a cBridge chain move.

What should I have ready before bridging?

First, confirm the asset is in your wallet on the source network. Then check that the bridge route supports that exact token, the source network, and the destination network. A matching ticker alone is not enough: routes depend on supported network and asset combinations.

Keep the source network’s native gas token in the wallet for transaction costs. An ERC-20 token may also need a separate token approval before the bridge deposit. Approval authorizes the contract to use the token; the deposit is a separate transaction. Native coins do not need this token approval.

Choose the destination address before starting. A transfer can arrive successfully while the destination wallet hides the token. Check that the wallet can display the destination token contract, so you can see the asset after delivery.

How do I prepare a bridge transfer?

Say you want to move USDC from Ethereum to Arbitrum. Start with your wallet connected to Ethereum, holding USDC and enough ETH for gas. cBridge is one place to check whether a route for that source, destination, and token is offered; availability depends on its supported routes.

  1. Select Ethereum as the source network, Arbitrum as the destination, and USDC as the token. You have now set the route to review.
  2. Enter the destination address and amount. Review the estimated receipt, fees, and token. The address identifies where the destination funds should arrive.
  3. If prompted, approve the token, then sign the bridge transfer. Approval authorizes token use; signing the transfer authorizes the transaction. Wait for delivery on Arbitrum and check that the destination wallet displays the received token.

What fees do I need to pay to bridge crypto?

You may need source-network gas for the on-chain transaction. An ERC-20 approval can require its own transaction and gas. Review the route’s displayed fees and estimated receipt before signing.

Do I need the same wallet on both networks?

You need a destination address that can receive the supported token. It can be the same address if your wallet supports both networks, but confirm the address and token display before signing.

Before you start, confirm the route and token, set the destination address, and keep enough source-network gas for the required transactions.
