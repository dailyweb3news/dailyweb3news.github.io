A non-custodial wallet for taking control of your crypto

A non-custodial wallet keeps the private key with you and signs transactions locally, so you control on-chain funds and can restore access with your recovery phrase.

The Onchain Brief EditorsNov 20, 2026 · 16:44 UTC3 min read

For an exchange user setting up a wallet, a non-custodial crypto wallet is software or a device that keeps your private key under your control and signs transactions locally. Control comes from holding the key, not from having an account.

A wallet uses your key to approve a transaction, then sends the signed transaction to the blockchain. Most wallets create a 12- or 24-word recovery phrase using BIP-39, a standard for turning those words into keys and addresses. The phrase can restore the wallet in another compatible app or device. The 0x protocol affects how a Matcha swap finds a trading route, while your wallet handles your approval and signature.

Your wallet signs; the blockchain holds the funds

Your coins stay on the blockchain at your address; the wallet app shows them and gives you a way to use them. Losing or deleting the app does not erase the coins. Restore the wallet with its recovery phrase, and a compatible wallet can derive the same keys and addresses.

An exchange account works differently: the exchange holds the keys and signs transactions for you. It can reset your account access. With a non-custodial wallet, only someone with the recovery phrase or private key can sign. That gives you control, along with responsibility for keeping the phrase safe.

Take a constructed example: you want to swap one token for another. In MetaMask, create a wallet and write its 12 words on paper. Connect MetaMask to Matcha swap, choose the tokens, and review the trade. When a wallet popup asks for approval, MetaMask signs locally with your key. The trading interface finds a route across liquidity sources; the signed transaction is what authorizes the swap from your address.

For a first setup, keep the phrase offline. Never share it with a support agent or enter it on a website. Anyone who gets the phrase can use it to drain the wallet. Type it only when restoring access in a wallet you trust. Keep two offline copies in separate places, such as written paper stored securely at home and another copy in a separate secure location.

Custodial vs non-custodial wallet: which is safer?

A custodial wallet puts the exchange in charge of the keys and account recovery. A non-custodial wallet gives you the keys, but you must protect the recovery phrase yourself. The safer choice depends on whether you can store the phrase carefully and keep it private.

What happens if I lose my recovery phrase?

No one can recover a lost phrase for you. If you still have access to the wallet, make and secure offline copies before losing that access. If you lose both the phrase and access to the wallet, you may lose the ability to sign for the funds at that address.

Start by creating a wallet and storing its phrase offline before connecting it to a trading interface. That gives you a way to restore access if the app or device is lost.
