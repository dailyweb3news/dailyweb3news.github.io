Manta Bridge deposits: what if you use the wrong network?

A wrong-network deposit may not be recoverable. The transaction details show what happened, what to check next and how to avoid repeating the mistake.

The Onchain Brief EditorsOct 14, 2026 · 13:24 UTC3 min read

A wrong-network deposit sent through a Manta Bridge transfer may not arrive where you expect, and it usually cannot be reversed once confirmed. The key detail is which blockchain received the transaction, not just the token name or wallet address. A deposit page may ask for a specific network; using another can leave the receiving service unable to credit the funds.

First, check the transaction record and identify the network used. If your goal is to move assets between chains, a Manta Bridge cross-chain bridge and swap service lets users move and swap crypto across different blockchains in one flow. That is a practical option when you have not sent the funds yet and need a cross-chain transfer.

What does a Manta Bridge wrong-network deposit mean?

It means the asset was sent on a blockchain that the receiving address or service did not specify. The same token can exist on several networks, and a matching address format does not prove that the receiving service can use funds sent on each one. A transfer between chains also needs a bridge step; sending directly to a deposit address does not perform that step for you.

For example, if a deposit service asks for a token on one network and you send the same-named token on another, the transaction can be confirmed on the second network while the service shows no deposit. The blockchain record and the service’s account balance are separate things.

Can a wrong-network crypto deposit be recovered?

Sometimes, but there is no general recovery switch. A confirmed blockchain transaction cannot normally be cancelled. Recovery depends on who controls the destination address and whether they can access the asset on the network where it arrived. If the address belongs to an exchange or another service, only that service can say whether it can recover or credit the funds.

Start with the transaction hash, the public identifier for a transfer. Check it on an explorer for the network actually used, then note the token, amount, destination address and confirmation status. Contact the receiving service through its official channel and give it those details. Do not send more funds to “unlock” a recovery or share your seed phrase; a seed phrase gives control of a wallet.

What should you check before sending through a bridge?

Before approving a transfer, compare the sending network, receiving network and token with the destination instructions. Confirm the receiving address, and make sure the destination can accept that asset on that chain. If any detail is unclear, pause and check it before signing.

  • Match the network on both sides of the transfer.
  • Check that the token and destination address are correct.
  • Keep the transaction hash after sending.
  • For a first transfer, consider sending a small amount if the service allows it.

A bridge can move assets between supported chains, but it does not make every address or token compatible with every network. The safest next step after a wrong-network transfer is to verify the transaction and ask the destination service what it can recover. If you have not sent the funds, check the route before you approve it.
