FBI links $6 billion Binance theft to North Korea

The FBI says North Korean hackers stole more than $6 billion from Binance, and is urging crypto firms to block wallets as investigators track the funds.

The Onchain Brief EditorsNov 3, 2026 · 18:26 UTC2 min read

The FBI says North Korea was behind the theft of more than $6 billion in digital assets from Binance, the world’s largest cryptocurrency exchange. The bureau linked the attack to a group it calls TraderTraitor. Its October 31 announcement says the theft happened on or about October 29.

The Associated Press reported that the theft happened within minutes and appears to be the largest crypto heist on record. Binance said more than $6 billion was taken. The FBI’s announcement did not give a more exact total.

How does the FBI say the stolen funds are moving?

The FBI says TraderTraitor has been converting the stolen assets mainly into Ether, the cryptocurrency used on the Ethereum network. It expects the group to move the funds through decentralised finance services and instant swap services. These tools let users trade or move crypto without relying on a traditional exchange.

The FBI, the US Secret Service and the IRS Criminal Investigation division urged crypto businesses to block transactions linked to wallet addresses the bureau identified. That request covers exchanges, blockchain tracking firms and services that help move crypto between networks.

What does the Binance theft have to do with Bybit?

The FBI says TraderTraitor was also behind the theft of about $1.5 billion from crypto exchange Bybit in 2025. The link puts the Binance attack in the context of a series of major crypto thefts attributed to North Korean hackers.

Binance said it had enough of the same digital currencies set aside to cover 90% of the missing assets and promised customers would be fully reimbursed, according to the AP. The FBI’s announcement focuses on tracking and blocking the stolen funds; it does not say how much has been recovered.

What remains uncertain about the FBI’s finding?

The FBI has publicly attributed the attack to North Korea, but the announcement does not describe how investigators identified the group or provide evidence in detail. The AP reported that President Donald Trump said investigators were still learning about the case and that North Korea’s role was not yet certain.

The FBI’s attribution is an official finding, not a public account of the full investigation. The bureau has asked private firms to help disrupt the movement of the funds while it continues to track them.
