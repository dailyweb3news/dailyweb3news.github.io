Binance says fake job interview aided $6.3B hack

Binance says a fake interview helped hackers reach its wallets before a $6.34 billion theft; it has frozen $2.85 billion, and its investigation is ongoing.

The Onchain Brief EditorsNov 4, 2026 · 06:40 UTC3 min read

Binance says attackers used a fake job interview as part of a months-long campaign that led to about $6.34 billion in digital assets being affected in an October 29 hack. The exchange’s November 3 incident report says the campaign combined social engineering, malware and access to internal systems.

The FBI has attributed the theft to North Korea, calling the activity “TraderTraitor.” Binance says its investigation is ongoing, and the report’s findings will receive an independent review.

How did a fake interview reach Binance’s wallet team?

In September, a person posing as a project director contacted a Binance software engineer through a legitimate job-search platform, according to the exchange. The engineer took two interviews for a role at a fictitious company. In the second, the supposed employer asked the engineer to download a coding assignment to a work computer.

The assignment contained malicious code that ran during setup. Binance says the malware evaded the engineer’s device security tools. The attackers later used the compromised computer and an internal AI tool to gain access to two other engineers’ systems.

The fake interview was one stage of the campaign. Binance says an earlier compromise began in June, when another engineer downloaded a tampered software installer while searching for an AI programming tool. The exchange detected that device and replaced it, but says the engineers working on wallet systems remained exposed.

How did the attackers move the crypto?

On October 29, the attackers used stolen credentials to access a third engineer’s computer, deploy a container and run a malicious script, Binance says. They then used an internal wallet signing service to authorize transfers outside the usual withdrawal process.

Five Binance hot wallets—wallets connected to the internet—sent 233.46 million XRP to an attacker-controlled address in four minutes. The attackers also moved other assets. Binance says the total value affected was about $6.34 billion.

Some transfers used Ethereum’s EIP-7702 system, which lets an account give a smart contract permission to carry out certain actions. Binance says the attackers used this to give a contract they controlled limited authority over a hot wallet. The exchange needed extra on-chain transactions to revoke that permission.

What remains unknown about Binance’s losses?

Binance says about $2.85 billion in assets had been frozen or intercepted by November 3, with the figure subject to reconciliation and price changes. It also says it had restored 98% of affected token assets by October 31 and would cover the financial impact for users.

The investigation is still underway. Binance says it is hiring independent security firms to review its findings. The FBI’s October 31 notice identifies North Korea as responsible, but Binance has not published a final technical postmortem.
