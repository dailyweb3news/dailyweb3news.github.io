Mayan vs deBridge routes for EVM-to-Solana transfers

Mayan vs deBridge: choose the Solana route that delivers more after fees for your token pair, then use token support and fill time to break a close quote.

The Onchain Brief EditorsNov 28, 2026 · 05:41 UTC3 min read

For an EVM-to-Solana transfer, pick the Mayan or deBridge quote that delivers more of your target token after fees. If the amounts are close, check that the route supports your token directly and compare its fill-time estimate.

How should you compare Mayan and deBridge quotes?

Mayan and deBridge both use solvers—parties that compete to fill cross-chain orders—so the best route can change with the token pair and quote. Mayan Swift runs a solver auction and uses Wormhole for cross-chain messaging. deBridge fills orders through DLN, where takers use their own liquidity to pay out on the destination chain.

Compare the quotes in this order:

  1. Choose the same source amount and destination token for both routes.
  2. Compare each route’s minimum received after fees, not just its headline rate.
  3. Check the estimated fill time and whether the target token is listed directly.

Mayan focuses on Solana routes and can swap into many SPL tokens in one step. deBridge DLN handles orders across many chains, including Solana, with takers paying out on the destination. A lifi bridge quote can put these routes side by side. The lifi bridge route comparison adds background on how an aggregator presents competing options.

Take an illustrative case: you send 1,000 USDC from Arbitrum to Solana. Suppose the Mayan quote shows a minimum of 997.80 USDC and deBridge shows 997.40 USDC. The difference is 40 cents, so choose Mayan for this quote, assuming both estimates use the same destination token and fee basis. The route name alone does not settle the choice; a later quote could favor deBridge.

There is one extra cost to plan for on Solana. The first time your wallet receives a particular SPL token, it needs an associated token account, or ATA, for that token. Creating it requires a small SOL rent deposit. So the first USDC transfer to a wallet may need some SOL available for setup; later USDC transfers to that same wallet do not need a new USDC account. The USDC amount received and the route can stay the same, while the first transfer costs more overall because the wallet setup is new.

When the quotes differ by more than a few cents, take the higher minimum received. When they are nearly equal, choose the route that lists your target token directly; use fill time to settle any remaining tie.

Related questions

For Arbitrum-to-Solana transfers, use the live estimate shown for your specific amount and token pair; timing can vary with the route and its fill.

How long does a transfer from Arbitrum to Solana take?

Use the fill-time estimate in the current quote. Mayan and deBridge rely on solvers or takers to fill orders, so the estimate for your amount and route is more useful than a general promise about speed.

Can I receive SOL directly when bridging USDC to Solana?

Only if the quote offers SOL as the destination token. Otherwise, choose a route that lists SOL directly or bridge USDC and swap after it arrives. Compare the final amount and any extra step before sending.

For your Arbitrum USDC transfer, compare both live minimum-received amounts and pick the higher one unless the quotes are nearly equal.
