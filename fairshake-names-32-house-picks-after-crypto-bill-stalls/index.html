Fairshake names 32 House picks after crypto bill stalls

Fairshake will back 32 House incumbents, with $6 million set for six races, after Senate lawmakers blocked the crypto market-structure bill in September.

The Onchain Brief EditorsOct 8, 2026 · 13:22 UTC2 min read

Crypto campaign group Fairshake says it will support 32 House incumbents in the November midterms, including six candidates with $1 million in planned spending each. The bipartisan slate comes after the Senate blocked a major crypto bill in September. The Associated Press reported that all 32 candidates backed the industry’s priority legislation, the CLARITY Act.

Which House candidates are set to get $1 million?

Fairshake plans to spend $1 million each to support three Democrats and three Republicans. The Democrats are Reps. Janelle Bynum of Oregon, Steven Horsford of Nevada and Derek Tran of California. The Republicans are Reps. French Hill of Arkansas, Bryan Steil of Wisconsin and Bill Huizenga of Michigan.

The group did not disclose how much it plans to spend on the other 26 candidates. Its full House spending total is therefore unknown. Fairshake and its affiliated political committees reported about $120 million in cash at the end of August, according to AP.

Why is Fairshake backing candidates from both parties?

The candidates share support for the CLARITY Act, which would set a federal regulatory framework for digital assets and divide oversight between regulators. Fairshake says it backs candidates based on crypto policy, regardless of party.

Its spokesperson, Geoff Vetter, told AP that the group and its affiliates spent $40 million supporting 29 Democrats and $31 million supporting 28 Republicans in primaries and special elections this year. Those figures cover earlier contests, not the new House slate.

What does the Senate bill’s failure leave unresolved?

The Senate blocked the CLARITY Act in September after lawmakers failed to agree on ethics rules, including how to address President Donald Trump’s crypto investments. The bill’s future is uncertain, and Fairshake has not said whether it will spend more in House or Senate races before November.

The group has separately pledged nearly $30 million against Democratic Senate candidate Sherrod Brown in Ohio. The wider crypto industry will look to the next Congress to revive legislation that stalled this year, but the election spending plan does not guarantee the bill will advance.
