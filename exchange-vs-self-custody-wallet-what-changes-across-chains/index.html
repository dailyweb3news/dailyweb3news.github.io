Exchange vs self-custody wallet: what changes across chains?

Exchange vs self-custody wallet for moving crypto between chains: an exchange pays from its wallet after deposit confirmations; a bridge moves funds from yours.

The Onchain Brief EditorsNov 19, 2026 · 04:31 UTC3 min read

An exchange withdrawal switches chains on the exchange’s ledger and pays out from its own wallet; a self-custody bridge moves funds from your wallet through contracts you sign. The key difference is custody: the exchange controls the funds during the move, while a bridge transfer starts with your wallet and requires you to handle the network steps.

What changes between an exchange withdrawal and a bridge?

An exchange route has two hops. First, you send crypto to a deposit address on the source network. The exchange waits for that deposit to reach its required confirmation count before it credits your account and opens withdrawal. Then you choose the destination network and ask the exchange to send funds from its wallet.

You need an exchange account, and the exchange holds custody while your deposit is credited and the withdrawal is prepared. The network you choose at withdrawal determines where the exchange sends the payout. The deposit and withdrawal are separate steps, with the exchange’s confirmation requirement between them.

A bridge route starts with funds in your own wallet. You connect the wallet, approve the token if needed, choose the destination network, and sign the transfer. You pay the source network’s gas yourself. The bridge’s contracts handle settlement, with the State Guardian Network involved in cBridge transfers.

cBridge is a cross-chain bridge that transfers supported assets between networks through pooled liquidity or token mappings. That means your move is initiated from your wallet and settled through the bridge, rather than by an exchange crediting your account and paying out later. The celer network connection matters because it is part of how this bridge transfer is settled.

How would a USDT move from Ethereum to BNB Chain work?

Say you hold USDT on Ethereum and want USDT on BNB Chain. With an exchange, you send the Ethereum USDT to the exchange’s Ethereum deposit address. You wait until the exchange records enough confirmations, then request a USDT withdrawal on BNB Chain. The exchange sends the payout from its own wallet on that network.

With cBridge, you start with USDT in your wallet on Ethereum, choose BNB Chain as the destination, and sign the transfer. The bridge settles the move through its contracts and State Guardian Network. It is one bridge transfer from your wallet, while the exchange route needs a deposit and a separate withdrawal. Token approval may also be needed before the transfer, and its gas comes from you.

Both routes depend on choosing the right asset and network at each step. On an exchange, check the deposit network first and match it to the network you send from; later, check the withdrawal network before submitting. With a bridge, check the source and destination networks in the wallet before signing, and keep enough source-chain funds to pay gas.

Use an exchange when you want its account-based deposit and withdrawal flow and accept that it holds custody during the process. Use a bridge when you want to move supported assets directly from your wallet and are ready to approve, sign, and pay source gas yourself. For this USDT move, decide which process fits, then verify Ethereum and BNB Chain before sending.
