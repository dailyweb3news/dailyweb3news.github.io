How to turn crypto into a restaking position

A restaking position starts with an asset the protocol accepts, then adds a service and operator choice; check conversion costs, exit terms and added risk.

The Onchain Brief EditorsOct 15, 2026 · 23:59 UTC3 min read

To convert crypto into a restaking position, first get an asset the restaking protocol accepts, then deposit it and choose which service and operator it will support. You may need to swap your current coin, but you may also be able to use a liquid staking token—a token that represents staked crypto. Restaking lets staked assets help secure another network service, with the chance of extra rewards and extra risk.

For example, someone holding a coin that a protocol does not accept may swap it for ETH or an accepted staking token. The route depends on the asset and network, so check both before trading. For a broader look at where a first swap can happen, see these QuickSwap routes for a first token swap. The right route for a restaking deposit still depends on the protocol’s accepted assets.

What crypto can you restake?

You can restake only assets the protocol supports. Some services accept ETH that is already staked through a validator; others accept liquid staking tokens or a protocol’s own receipt token. These options are not interchangeable. A receipt token is a claim managed by its issuing service, and its value or redemption terms can differ from the underlying asset.

Before converting, find the protocol’s deposit page and confirm:

  • The exact token and network it accepts.
  • Whether you need the original asset or a staking receipt token.
  • Any swap fee, network fee, and price difference you might face while trading.

Use the same network shown by the deposit page. A token with the same name on another network may not work. Check the token address too, since copycat tokens can use familiar names.

How do you choose a restaking position?

After you have the accepted asset, choose the service it will help secure and the operator that runs the relevant software. The service sets the rules for the work being done. The operator carries it out. Rewards, fees, and withdrawal terms depend on the protocol and service, so read those details before confirming a deposit.

Restaking can add another condition under which your stake may be penalized. This is called slashing: a loss of stake for breaking a service’s rules or failing its requirements. The risks vary by service and protocol. A position that earns rewards from more than one source can also involve more contracts and more parties to trust.

How do you convert and deposit safely?

For most readers, the simpler route is to use a supported asset they already hold, if the protocol accepts it. That avoids an extra swap and its fees. If you must convert, compare the amount you expect to receive after fees and price movement with the deposit amount the protocol requires.

Then connect your wallet, check the deposit details, and approve only the token and amount needed. A token approval lets a contract move tokens from your wallet; some wallets let you limit it to a set amount. Review the final transaction before signing. Keep enough of the network’s fee token to pay for the deposit and later transactions.

Restaking is not just a currency conversion. You are moving an asset into a position with new rules, operators, and exit steps. Know what you hold, what service it backs, and how you can withdraw before you deposit.
