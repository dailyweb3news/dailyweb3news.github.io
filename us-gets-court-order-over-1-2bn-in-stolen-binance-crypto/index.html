US gets court order over $1.2bn in stolen Binance crypto

The Justice Department says a court has authorized seizure or restraint of more than $1.2 billion from the Binance theft, as recovery efforts continue.

The Onchain Brief EditorsNov 22, 2026 · 16:54 UTC2 min read

The U.S. Justice Department says it has court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance. The department announced the action on Nov. 12, saying the funds are part of a larger pool already frozen by U.S. agencies and private firms.

In its announcement, the Justice Department said more than $2.7 billion of the stolen assets had been frozen so far. The court authorization covers more than $1.2 billion of that amount. Restraining funds blocks them from being moved while legal steps proceed.

What happened to Binance’s funds

The theft took place on or around Oct. 29. The Justice Department said North Korean military members, whom the FBI calls TraderTraitor, stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from the exchange.

The FBI has attributed the attack to the group. The department said U.S. agencies worked with virtual asset service providers, companies that handle crypto transfers and storage, to freeze funds before they moved beyond law enforcement’s reach.

The Associated Press report said the theft was estimated at about $6.3 billion. It also noted that the U.S. government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge in these cases. A judge then decides who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds.

Recovery work is still under way

The Justice Department said efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the stolen assets are continuing. It said TraderTraitor actors have been laundering funds through crypto bridges, mixers and exchanges. Bridges move assets between blockchains; mixers are services designed to obscure where crypto came from.

The court authorization does not by itself confirm that the funds have been returned to Binance or its customers. The department’s announcement describes the assets as subject to seizure or restraint, and says the recovery process is ongoing.

The Binance theft was announced alongside a separate U.S. action against a North Korean scheme called FaceStealer. The department said that scheme stole more than $400 million in cryptocurrency over the past 18 months. It described the operation as separate from the Binance case.
