Binance says fake job interview led to $6.34 billion hack

Binance says a fake recruitment process helped hackers reach its wallet systems before a $6.34 billion theft, with most affected tokens since replenished.

The Onchain Brief EditorsNov 6, 2026 · 18:10 UTC2 min read

Binance says hackers used a fake job interview to reach its wallet systems before stealing $6.34 billion in crypto on Oct. 29. The exchange described the attack as a months-long operation. In its account of the breach, CoinDesk reported Binance’s timeline, including the fake recruitment process and the later use of an internal AI tool.

In September, a software engineer was contacted on a legitimate job-hunting platform by someone posing as a project director at a fictitious company, according to Binance. The engineer had a video interview with a supposed HR representative, then a second interview with someone claiming to be the company’s CEO. The coding assignment they were asked to download contained malware, or software designed to secretly harm or access a device.

How did the fake interview reach Binance’s wallet team?

Binance said attackers used the engineer’s compromised computer and an internal AI tool on Oct. 20 to gain access to two other employees’ computers. Those employees worked on Binance’s wallet infrastructure. The company had previously detected malware on another engineer’s device in June, replaced the computer and changed related credentials, but said the employee’s role and colleagues remained exposed.

On Oct. 29, attackers used stolen credentials to deploy a malicious script. Binance said the script gave them access to its wallet signing service, which approves transactions. They also used Ethereum’s EIP-7702, a feature that lets a wallet grant another party permission to carry out certain actions. CoinDesk reported that this allowed unauthorized transfers from a Binance hot wallet, a wallet connected to online systems.

How much of the stolen crypto has been recovered?

Binance said about $2.85 billion had been frozen or intercepted. It also said 98% of affected tokens had been replenished with company funds by Oct. 31. The exchange said it would bring in independent security experts to review the incident. BleepingComputer’s report said Binance was continuing its investigation and had rebuilt affected systems, replaced compromised devices and revoked stolen credentials.

Who does Binance say was behind the attack?

Binance said the FBI attributed the attack to North Korea. The exchange has not publicly identified the people behind the fake company or the job interview. The FBI attribution identifies who authorities believe was responsible; it does not establish the identities of the individuals who contacted the engineer.
