US gets court order over $1.2bn in stolen Binance crypto

The Justice Department won court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2bn tied to the Binance hack, part of $2.7bn frozen so far.

The Onchain Brief EditorsNov 23, 2026 · 00:37 UTC2 min read

The US Justice Department has court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, after government agencies and private companies froze more than $2.7 billion in funds linked to the theft. The FBI said the theft was about $6.3 billion. The Associated Press report said the order covers funds within that frozen total.

What the FBI says happened

The FBI says North Korean hackers were responsible for the attack on or around October 29, 2026. In its October 31 alert, it calls the activity “TraderTraitor.” The FBI said the hackers were converting stolen assets mainly into Ether, a cryptocurrency, and expected them to move the funds through DeFi and instant swap services to launder them.

The FBI urged companies that operate blockchain nodes, exchanges, DeFi services and other crypto platforms to block transactions linked to the listed addresses. Its alert includes wallet addresses that the agency says hold, or have held, stolen assets.

How much has been frozen?

The $1.2 billion covered by the court authorization is part of more than $2.7 billion in stolen funds that the US government and private companies have frozen together, according to AP. A freeze prevents access to funds. The court order allows authorities to seek seizure or restraint of the portion it covers.

The figures describe different stages of recovery. The total stolen is about $6.3 billion; the frozen amount is more than $2.7 billion; and the order concerns more than $1.2 billion of that frozen sum. The reports do not say the entire stolen amount has been recovered.

Who gets the seized funds?

The court authorization does not settle who owns the money. AP reported that the government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then decides who has a valid claim to recovered funds. The reporting does not state that victims have already received any of the cryptocurrency.
