Zcash activates NU7, targeting 25-second blocks

Zcash activated NU7 at block 3,543,405, cutting its target block interval from 75 seconds to 25 and setting new limits for shielded activity and fees.

The Onchain Brief EditorsNov 9, 2026 · 02:14 UTC3 min read

Zcash activated its NU7 network upgrade at block 3,543,405, cutting the target time between blocks from 75 seconds to 25 and changing how private transactions and fees work. CoinDesk reported the activation at 02:34 UTC on Friday, Nov. 6.

A block records transactions on the network. More frequent blocks can mean faster first confirmations, including for shielded payments, which hide the sender, recipient and amount. But services such as exchanges set their own confirmation rules, so the upgrade does not guarantee that every payment will be available to use sooner.

How much faster are Zcash blocks now?

NU7 sets a 25-second target between blocks, one third of the former 75-second target. The target is not a promise that every block will arrive on schedule: CoinDesk reported that the 225 blocks after activation averaged about 39 seconds as mining difficulty adjusted.

The shorter target could reduce the average wait for a first confirmation to about 25 seconds once the network settles at that pace. For a service that waits for three confirmations, the expected wait could fall from nearly four minutes to about 75 seconds. Actual timing depends on block production and each service’s policy.

What changes for private payments and wallets?

NU7 limits shielded activity, meaning transactions that keep payment details private, in each block. The Zcash Foundation’s Zebra 7.0.0 release notes say these limits more than double Orchard’s capacity, from 2.9 to 6.6 transactions per second, while cutting the worst-case data a spammer could make light wallets download by about 37%.

Wallets scan the chain to find payments meant for them. Limiting shielded activity helps contain that scanning burden as blocks arrive more often. The Foundation says most wallets do not need significant changes, though node operators had to upgrade compatible software to remain on the same chain after activation.

What happens to transaction fees and old Sprout funds?

Miners keep 40% of transaction fees under NU7. The other 60% is taken out of circulation and placed in a reserve for future block rewards. Reissuance from that reserve is scheduled to begin in 2031; the upgrade keeps Zcash’s 21 million ZEC supply cap.

NU7 also makes version 4 transactions invalid. Since transfers from Sprout, Zcash’s original privacy pool, can only use that format, any ZEC left there became unspendable at activation. CoinDesk reported about 12,224 ZEC remained in Sprout and that no recovery plan had been approved as of Friday.
