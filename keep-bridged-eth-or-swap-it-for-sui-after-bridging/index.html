Keep bridged ETH or swap it for SUI after bridging

Keep bridged ETH on Sui or swap it for SUI? Hold ETH for Ethereum exposure, or swap some for SUI to cover gas and match your plans.

The Onchain Brief EditorsNov 26, 2026 · 22:40 UTC3 min read

For most people, the practical choice is to keep bridged ETH and hold a small amount of SUI for gas. Every Sui transaction needs gas paid in SUI, as the Sui gas-fee documentation explains. If you may send the funds back to Ethereum, holding ETH also avoids swapping to SUI and back; Suibridge is relevant to that return route.

What changes when you swap?

Holding bridged ETH keeps your price exposure tied to ETH. Swapping it for SUI shifts that exposure to SUI, so the choice depends partly on which asset you want to hold while your funds are on Sui.

Gas is a separate practical need. With no exchange handling it for you, you need SUI in your wallet before you can submit transactions. That includes swaps and other onchain actions. Without SUI for gas, those transactions cannot run.

Say you expect to use most of the funds on Sui, but want to keep some ETH exposure. You could swap only the amount of ETH you want to convert and leave enough SUI for gas. The right gas balance depends on the transactions you plan to make, so there is no single amount that fits everyone.

The return plan matters because a swap to SUI and back to ETH adds two swap spreads: one on each conversion. Bridge gas is an additional cost. If the funds are bound for Ethereum, keeping them as bridged ETH avoids those two swap spreads. Bridged ETH on Sui is its own coin type; it returns to Ethereum through Suibridge.

Which option fits your plan?

Holding all the bridged ETH suits you if you want to keep ETH exposure and do not need to make Sui transactions yet. Remember that you will still need SUI for gas before you can move or swap the ETH on Sui.

Swapping all of it for SUI suits you if you want SUI exposure long term and do not plan to return the funds to Ethereum. It also means you no longer hold that amount as ETH, and each swap shifts your exposure and incurs spread.

Holding ETH plus a small SUI balance for gas suits you if you want to keep ETH exposure while using Sui. It gives you SUI to pay transaction fees without converting all your bridged ETH. The balance should reflect what you expect to do, not an assumed standard amount.

For funds you may send back to Ethereum, hold the bridged ETH and swap only enough for gas. The deciding constraint is whether you want the funds to return to Ethereum; if you want SUI long term instead, swap.
