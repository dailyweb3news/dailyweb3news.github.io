Pick the token you need before you bridge

Choose the destination token before bridging: a route that delivers the asset you need can spare a second swap, extra fees and avoidable price risk on arrival.

The Onchain Brief EditorsOct 15, 2026 · 10:24 UTC3 min read

To avoid a second swap when bridging tokens, choose a route that delivers the asset you plan to use on the destination chain. A bridge moves value between chains, but it may deliver a different version of a token from the one an app accepts. Checking the destination token before you send can save a trade, its fee and the price movement that may happen while you make it.

Why can a bridge leave you needing another swap?

A bridge transfers tokens between blockchains, but the tokens on each chain are separate assets. The same coin may have a native version and one or more bridged versions, issued to represent coins moved from elsewhere. A wallet can show a token that looks right by name while an app accepts only a different version.

Some routes also swap tokens as part of the transfer; others deliver the same token type on the destination chain. The choice depends on the route and the asset you select. For a step-by-step explanation of cross-chain transfers and swaps, see this AcrossBridge guide to moving tokens. The key is to check what the route promises to deliver, not just the token you start with.

How do you choose a route that delivers the right token?

Start with the destination app or activity, then identify the exact token it needs. Check its network and token address where available; matching names and ticker symbols alone do not prove that two tokens are the same asset. Then compare bridge routes and confirm the output token each one lists.

Before confirming, check these details:

  • Destination chain: Make sure the route ends on the network where you want to use the funds.
  • Output token: Confirm the token name and, if shown, its contract address.
  • Amount received: Compare the estimated output after fees, not only the amount sent.
  • Route steps: See whether the route includes a swap and what token that swap produces.

A route that combines a bridge and a swap may be simpler than doing both yourself, but its quoted output can be lower because fees and trading costs are included. A route with no swap may cost less, yet leave you holding a token that needs another trade before use. Compare the final amount in the token you actually need.

What should you check before sending?

Check the network, output token and quoted amount on the final confirmation screen. Keep enough of the destination chain’s gas token—the coin used to pay transaction fees—to make a later transaction if needed. A bridge transfer can take time, and the final amount may change if the route’s price estimate moves before execution.

For a small first transfer, use an amount you can afford to have delayed while you confirm it arrives as expected. If the route cannot show which token you will receive, pause and look up that detail before proceeding. In most cases, choosing the correct destination asset up front is the simpler path: it avoids an extra decision and trade after the transfer.
