Which Gnosis Bridge limit stopped my transfer?

Bridge daily limit vs execution daily limit: one can revert a deposit; the other can stop arrival after it mines. Find the failed step and choose the right recovery.

The Onchain Brief EditorsNov 28, 2026 · 08:48 UTC3 min read

Read the transaction state first: a reverted deposit points to dailyLimit; a mined deposit with no arrival points to executionDailyLimit. Each mediator tracks its own daily total for each token, so the limit on one chain does not tell you whether the other chain can release the transfer.

Gnosis Bridge is a concrete case of this split: Omnibridge and Arbitrary Message Bridge use mediators to move tokens and messages between Ethereum and Gnosis Chain. That cross-chain routing makes the bridge to gnosis chain a useful example of why the sending and receiving limits must be checked separately.

Which chain's limit did the transfer hit?

dailyLimit is checked by the sending chain's mediator when you deposit. It caps the amount that can leave that chain per day for a token. A breach reverts the deposit, so the tokens stay in your wallet. There is no destination transfer to recover.

Say you submit a large deposit and the transaction reverts. Check its receipt in GnosisScan or Etherscan. Then use the sending mediator's Read Contract view to compare that token's dailyLimit with totalSpentPerDay. These values are set per token by bridge governance and vary. Retry when the daily total allows it, or send a smaller amount. Check the current values again before resending.

executionDailyLimit is checked by the receiving chain's mediator when it processes the incoming message. It caps what that chain can release per day for the token. The deposit can be mined successfully, yet the message can fail on arrival because the receiving mediator has reached its limit.

For a mined deposit with no arriving tokens, trace the transaction and message in GnosisScan or Etherscan. Confirm that the source transaction succeeded and the destination execution failed. In the receiving mediator's Read Contract view, compare the token's executionDailyLimit with totalExecutedPerDay. Once the failed message is visible, call requestFailedMessageFix on the source-side mediator to refund. A new attempt needs room under the relevant daily limit; check both sides before sending again.

What else can block a bridge transfer?

What is maxPerTx on a bridge?

maxPerTx is a per-transfer ceiling. It differs from a daily limit, which measures the token total used over the day. A transfer can fit under maxPerTx and still exceed the remaining daily allowance.

Can splitting a transfer get under a bridge limit?

Splitting may bring each part under maxPerTx, but it does not create more daily capacity. The parts still count toward the token's daily total on the sending chain and, when executed, on the receiving chain. Read both mediators' values before resending.

  • A reverted deposit means the source-side dailyLimit blocked it; funds stay in the wallet.
  • A mined deposit with failed arrival points to the receiving-side executionDailyLimit; use requestFailedMessageFix on the source mediator to refund.
  • Compare each per-token limit with its daily total before another attempt.
