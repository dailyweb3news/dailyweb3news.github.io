Why Manta Pacific withdrawals can take days

A Manta Pacific withdrawal can wait days while Ethereum checks the rollup’s record. The withdrawal stages explain the delay and what to check before you start.

The Onchain Brief EditorsOct 22, 2026 · 11:19 UTC3 min read

A Manta Pacific withdrawal can take days because the standard route waits for a security window before Ethereum releases the funds. Manta Pacific is a layer 2, a network that processes transactions alongside Ethereum and later settles them there. That makes deposits and withdrawals work differently: a deposit can arrive after a short confirmation, while a withdrawal has extra checks before the funds return to Ethereum.

Why does a Manta Pacific withdrawal have a waiting period?

The standard withdrawal route gives people time to challenge a transaction record that may be wrong. Manta Pacific uses an optimistic rollup, which treats transaction records as valid unless someone disputes them. The wait gives that challenge process time to work before the withdrawal is treated as final on Ethereum.

Manta Network has described a three-day challenge period for withdrawals through its native bridge. The exact time to receive funds can be longer because the challenge period is only one stage of the trip. A withdrawal also needs to be started and later completed.

What happens between starting and receiving the withdrawal?

Starting the withdrawal records your request on Manta Pacific; it does not send the funds straight to your Ethereum wallet. The network must make the withdrawal record available for checking on Ethereum. After the security window passes, you may need to complete a final claim transaction there.

For a practical walkthrough of the steps, see this guide to the Manta Bridge withdrawal process. The route and its displayed status matter: “started” does not mean “received.”

  • Start: You request the withdrawal on Manta Pacific.
  • Wait: The withdrawal passes through the route’s security window.
  • Claim: You complete any final step on Ethereum and check that the funds arrived.

Each on-chain step may also need a network confirmation. Keep enough ETH for gas on the network where a transaction or claim is required.

Can you make the withdrawal faster?

Some routes may offer faster settlement, but the time and conditions depend on the route shown when you transfer. Manta has also described fast-finality work intended to shorten the wait. That does not mean every withdrawal automatically finishes in minutes, so check the live estimate and whether the route asks you to claim funds later.

A faster route can change the costs or process. Compare the estimated arrival time, fees and final amount before confirming. If you choose the standard route, plan around the security window and keep the transaction details so you can check each stage.

The main takeaway is simple: days of waiting can be part of the native withdrawal’s security process, not a sign that the transfer is stuck. If the status stops advancing or the expected claim step does not appear, check the transaction record and the bridge’s current instructions.
