Bitcoin rebounds after midterm dip as Binance hack weighs

Bitcoin briefly hit a six-week low after Democrats took Congress, then recovered as stocks turned higher; Binance’s $6.3 billion hack kept pressure on confidence.

The Onchain Brief EditorsNov 5, 2026 · 09:01 UTC2 min read

Bitcoin briefly fell to a six-week low after Democrats won control of both chambers of the US Congress in the November 4 midterm elections, then recovered as stocks turned higher. The result raised concern about the crypto industry’s push for faster deregulation, while a $6.3 billion hack at Binance added to market worries. Reuters’ report on the market swing said bitcoin was up 0.7% at $78,354 by 0841 GMT on Wednesday, and ether had gained 1.4% to $2,323.

Why did the election move crypto prices?

Democrats took control of the Senate and House, a shift that could slow efforts to ease rules for digital assets. Reuters described the election result as a potential setback for an industry that has sought faster deregulation. The outcome does not by itself change crypto laws, and some Democrats have also supported a light-touch approach to regulation.

Prices rose again as US stock futures recovered from overnight losses. Bitcoin had fallen as much as a six-week low before that rebound. The movement came amid broader weakness: Reuters reported bitcoin was down around 41% from its record high above $122,000 in October 2025, and the total crypto market had lost about $300 billion over the previous month.

What happened at Binance?

Binance said attackers compromised its hot wallets, which are online wallets used to move crypto, and stole tokens worth about $6.3 billion. In its security incident update, the exchange said about $3 billion had been frozen on-chain and it would absorb the financial impact so users would not bear the loss. The investigation was still underway, and Binance said it was working to establish the full attack sequence.

The theft shook confidence beyond the exchange. Reuters reported that bitcoin investment funds saw $884 million in net outflows for the week, their largest weekly outflow since early July. Binance customers had also withdrawn a net $4.72 billion by Tuesday after the exchange reopened over the weekend.

Is the market still falling?

Not across the whole session: bitcoin and ether had recovered by Wednesday morning, tracking the turn in stocks. The figures show a rebound after an overnight slide, not that the election or the hack had stopped weighing on sentiment. Reuters cited a HashKey researcher who said investors were reassessing security and custody risks, as well as whether political change could stall crypto legislation. How the new Congress will handle those bills remains unclear.
