How to Use super bridge From Your Own Wallet

A wallet-to-wallet bridge moves crypto between chains, but the source network, destination, fees and final confirmation all matter before you send funds.

The Onchain Brief EditorsOct 14, 2026 · 09:29 UTC3 min read

To use super bridge from your own wallet, choose the asset and source and destination chains, then send the required transaction from the wallet you control. A bridge moves value between blockchains, which are separate networks with their own transaction records. Some services also swap the asset during the move, so you can receive a different token on the destination chain.

First check what you need at the end: the token, network and wallet address that should receive it. When the move also requires a swap, use super bridge: it lets users move and swap crypto across blockchains in one flow. You still need to make sure the destination asset and network fit what you plan to do next.

What do you need before using super bridge?

You need a wallet with the asset you plan to send and enough of the source chain’s native token to pay its transaction fee. That fee is charged by the network for processing the transaction. The wallet you connect is the one that will approve the transfer, so check its account and network before continuing.

Write down the destination network and receiving address, or confirm that the connected wallet will receive the funds on that network. Addresses can look similar across chains, but a transfer sent to the wrong network may be difficult or impossible to recover. If you are moving funds to use a particular app, check which token and chain that app accepts.

How does a crypto bridge transfer work?

A bridge coordinates a transfer from one chain to another. Depending on how it works, the service may lock or collect the asset on the source chain and arrange for a corresponding asset to become available on the destination chain. A swap changes one token into another; it is a separate operation that some services combine with the bridge transfer.

The basic steps are:

  • Connect the wallet that holds the asset.
  • Select the source chain, asset and amount, then choose the destination chain and asset.
  • Review the quoted amount and any fee details shown before approving.
  • Approve the transaction in your wallet, then wait for the transfer to complete.

A wallet may ask you to approve token spending before it can submit a transfer. That approval gives a contract permission to use the specified token. Read the wallet prompt and confirm that the asset and amount match what you intend to move. The transfer may then need to be processed on the source chain and the destination chain before the funds appear.

What should you check after the transfer?

Check the destination wallet on the destination network. A completed source transaction does not always mean the funds are already available on the other chain; the bridge may need more time to finish the second part. If the asset does not appear, confirm the selected destination network and token before trying another transfer.

For most readers, the simplest route is to move only what they need and keep enough native token on the source chain for fees. A bridge can save the extra step of arranging a separate swap when both are needed. The trade-off is that a cross-chain move has more stages than a transfer on one chain, so check each choice before signing.
