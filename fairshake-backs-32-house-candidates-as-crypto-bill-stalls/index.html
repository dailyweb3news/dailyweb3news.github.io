Fairshake backs 32 House candidates as crypto bill stalls

Fairshake will back 32 House candidates, six with $1 million each, as the Senate’s stalled CLARITY Act leaves crypto rules unresolved before November.

The Onchain Brief EditorsOct 5, 20262 min read

Crypto political group Fairshake says it will support 32 House candidates in November’s midterm elections, including six who will each get $1 million in planned spending. The bipartisan slate gives the industry a route to support lawmakers after its main bill stalled in the Senate, The Associated Press reported.

Who is on Fairshake’s slate?

The list includes 19 Republicans and 13 Democrats. The six candidates set to receive $1 million each are Democrats Janelle Bynum of Oregon, Steven Horsford of Nevada and Derek Tran of California, and Republicans French Hill of Arkansas, Bryan Steil of Wisconsin and Bill Huizenga of Michigan.

Fairshake did not say how much it plans to spend on the other 26 candidates. The group and its affiliated political action committees, or PACs, reported about $120 million in cash at the end of August, according to the AP.

All 32 candidates supported the industry’s priority legislation. Fairshake spokesperson Geoff Vetter told the AP that the group backs candidates in both parties based on their crypto policy positions. He also said Fairshake and affiliated groups had won 53 of the 57 races they entered this election cycle.

What happened to the CLARITY Act?

The Senate did not advance the CLARITY Act in September after Democratic senators helped block it, the AP reported. The bill aimed to set federal rules for digital assets, including how regulators divide oversight. The failed vote leaves that framework unresolved as voters choose the next Congress.

The House slate comes alongside a much larger Senate effort. Fairshake announced plans in September to spend nearly $30 million against Democrat Sherrod Brown in Ohio. Brown is running against Republican Sen. Jon Husted. The group had not said whether it would put more money into House or Senate races before November.

Will Fairshake spend more before the election?

That remains unclear. The AP reported that Fairshake would not say whether it plans additional spending in either chamber. The six $1 million commitments show where it has disclosed specific amounts, but they do not reveal the full cost of the 32-candidate effort.

Fairshake previously backed Senate candidates from both parties. In 2024, its network spent about $10 million each supporting Democrats Ruben Gallego in Arizona and Elissa Slotkin in Michigan. That record shows the group has crossed party lines before, while its current House slate ties support to candidates who backed legislation important to the crypto industry.
