How to check an unclaimed Polygon withdrawal

A Polygon withdrawal can be pending, ready to claim or complete; use the original wallet and transaction record to find its status and next step safely.

The Onchain Brief EditorsOct 4, 20263 min read

To check an unclaimed Polygon withdrawal, find its transaction in the bridge you used and see whether it is waiting, ready for a claim or complete. A withdrawal is a transfer from Polygon to another chain, often Ethereum. Some routes need a second transaction on the destination chain before the funds arrive. The first transaction alone may not finish the trip.

Where can I find my Polygon withdrawal?

Start with the wallet account and bridge route used to make the withdrawal. In Polygon Portal, connect that wallet and look in transaction history; the page lists Native and Socket bridge transactions. If you used another route, check its own history too. A transaction may not appear in the Portal if it used a different service.

Open the withdrawal record and note the transaction hash, the long code that identifies a blockchain transaction. Check that hash on the explorer for the source chain, then confirm the destination address and token match your plan. A record marked failed means the withdrawal did not complete on that chain. A confirmed source transaction can still need another step. For a closer look at how the routes differ, see this guide to Polygon Bridge withdrawal paths.

Why is my withdrawal still waiting?

The next step depends on the route and asset. In a common PoS bridge withdrawal, the Polygon transaction is recorded first. The bridge then waits for a checkpoint, a record of Polygon activity posted to Ethereum, before the exit can be claimed there. Other routes use a challenge period or a different process. So “pending” does not by itself tell you that something went wrong.

Look for a status such as “ready to claim” or “action required” in the route’s interface. If it is ready, the interface should show the destination chain and the transaction needed to complete the withdrawal. That claim transaction may require destination-chain gas. If the status is still waiting, compare the source transaction with the route’s stated steps rather than sending another withdrawal.

What should I check before claiming?

Before signing, make sure the wallet and network shown in the prompt match the withdrawal record. Read the token, amount and destination address. Then check whether a claim transaction has already been sent; if it has, look up that transaction on the destination chain. The key checks are:

  • Source transaction: confirmed, pending or failed.
  • Route status: waiting, ready to claim or complete.
  • Destination details: correct chain, token and address.
  • Claim transaction: sent and confirmed, or still needed.

If the source transaction failed, the destination claim cannot complete from that attempt. If the claim failed, check its error and gas settings before retrying through the bridge interface. Never share a recovery phrase or sign a transaction prompted by a stranger claiming to help. When the source and destination records disagree, save both transaction hashes and contact the route’s official support channel.
