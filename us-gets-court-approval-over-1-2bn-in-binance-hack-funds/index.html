US gets court approval over $1.2bn in Binance hack funds

US authorities have court approval to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of Binance hack funds, part of a wider effort to freeze $2.7 billion.

The Onchain Brief EditorsNov 14, 2026 · 02:50 UTC2 min read

US authorities have court approval to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, the Justice Department said Thursday. The Justice Department announcement says the amount is part of more than $2.7 billion in stolen funds frozen through joint government and industry efforts. The Associated Press reported that the FBI described the action as a seizure.

What the court approval covers

The $1.2 billion is part of the larger pool of frozen assets, not an additional recovery on top of it. The Justice Department says it has obtained court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of the funds. A restraint can prevent assets from being moved while legal proceedings continue.

The department did not say that the cryptocurrency has been returned to Binance or to customers. It said efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the assets are ongoing. Forfeiture is the legal process for transferring property to the government or another rightful owner.

How the Binance theft unfolded

The Justice Department says members of North Korea’s military, whom the FBI calls TraderTraitor, stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance on or around Oct. 29. The agency did not give a breakdown of the stolen assets or say which specific coins make up the court-authorized amount.

Authorities and private companies have worked with virtual asset service providers, such as crypto exchanges, to block the stolen funds. The department says TraderTraitor actors continue to move assets through crypto bridges, mixers and exchanges. Bridges move assets between blockchains; mixers combine transactions to make funds harder to trace.

What remains unresolved

The Justice Department says the investigation and recovery work continue. Its announcement does not identify the court orders or say how much of the $1.2 billion has already been physically transferred into government control. The AP reported that the FBI said it had seized more than $1.2 billion, while also noting the department’s description of court authorization to seize or restrain that amount.

The two figures describe different stages of the response: more than $2.7 billion has been frozen overall, while the government has court approval to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of it. The department has not said when the forfeiture process will finish or who will ultimately receive the recovered funds.
