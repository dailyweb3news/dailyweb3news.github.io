Bridge aggregators: how they widen your route choice

A bridge aggregator compares bridges and swaps for one transfer, so you can weigh route options when a single bridge lacks the token or liquidity you need.

The Onchain Brief EditorsNov 28, 2026 · 06:50 UTC2 min read

A bridge aggregator compares routes across bridges and decentralised exchanges (DEXs) so you can choose how to move tokens between chains. A single bridge offers only its own routes, tokens and available liquidity, while an aggregator can quote several options for the same transfer.

What happens when you use an aggregator?

You choose the token and chain you are sending from, and the token and chain you want to receive. The aggregator gathers route options, which may combine more than one service:

  • A swap on the source chain
  • A bridge transfer
  • A swap on the destination chain

These parts connect into one route: the source swap can turn your starting token into one the bridge accepts, the bridge moves value across chains, and the destination swap can convert it into the token you want. The aggregator quotes routes through bridges and DEXs, then runs the route you select.

Jumper bridge is built on LI.FI, which routes through services such as Across, Stargate and Circle CCTP. For more on how this works for a transfer, Jumper bridge covers finding routes through supported bridges and swaps.

A single bridge has a narrower choice. Across, for instance, provides routes through its own bridge. If that bridge does not have the token or liquidity needed for your transfer, it may not offer a suitable quote. An aggregator can surface another bridge or include a swap to make a route possible.

How to compare the routes

Say you want to move USDC from Arbitrum to Base. An aggregator can show quotes using Across, Stargate and Circle CCTP side by side, with the estimated fee and time for each route. The routes may differ in the services they use, so compare the whole route rather than the bridge name alone.

The first route listed is the aggregator’s ranked pick. That ranking is a starting point, not a promise that it is the cheapest or fastest choice. Change the sort to cheapest or fastest and the order may change; a different route can become the best fit for what matters to you.

Before confirming, compare the fee and estimated time, then check which route you are selecting. Cheapest may suit a transfer where cost matters most. Fastest may suit one where timing matters more. The useful choice depends on the transfer you need to make.
