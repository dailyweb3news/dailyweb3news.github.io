Fairshake backs 32 House candidates as crypto bill stalls

Fairshake will back 32 House candidates, with $1 million each for six, as the crypto market bill remains stalled in the Senate before November's midterms.

The Onchain Brief EditorsOct 10, 2026 · 22:11 UTC2 min read

Fairshake plans to support 32 House candidates in November’s midterms, including six who will each get $1 million in spending, as the crypto industry looks to elect allies after a major bill stalled in the Senate. The slate includes 19 Republicans and 13 Democrats. The Associated Press report on Fairshake’s House slate said all 32 candidates had supported legislation that was a top industry priority.

Which candidates will get the $1 million?

Fairshake named three Democrats: Reps. Janelle Bynum of Oregon, Steven Horsford of Nevada and Derek Tran of California. The three Republicans are Reps. French Hill of Arkansas, Bryan Steil of Wisconsin and Bill Huizenga of Michigan.

The group did not disclose how much it plans to spend on the other 26 candidates. It also did not say whether it will put more money into House or Senate races before November. Fairshake and its affiliated PACs reported about $120 million in cash at the end of August, according to the AP.

Geoff Vetter, a Fairshake spokesperson, told the AP that the group and its affiliates spent $40 million supporting 29 Democrats and $31 million supporting 28 Republicans in primaries and special elections this year. Vetter said Fairshake backs candidates from both parties who support crypto.

What happened to the crypto bill?

The candidates supported the CLARITY Act, a bill intended to set federal rules for digital assets and divide oversight between regulators. Senate Democrats blocked it in September. Opponents said it did not adequately address President Donald Trump’s crypto investments, the AP reported in its coverage of the Senate vote.

The industry hoped the bill would give crypto businesses more certainty about which rules apply to them. Its failure leaves the effort to pass a regulatory framework to the next Congress, according to the AP.

Where could Fairshake spend next?

Fairshake has already pledged nearly $30 million against Democratic Senate candidate Sherrod Brown in Ohio, who is running against Republican Sen. Jon Husted. The group targeted Brown after Senate Democrats helped block the bill.

It remains unclear how Fairshake will use the rest of its reported cash before the election. The six House commitments make up $6 million of its support for the slate; the group has not given a full spending breakdown for all 32 races.
