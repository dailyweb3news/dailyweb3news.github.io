US wins court backing to seize $1.2bn in Binance theft

US authorities have court approval to seize or restrain over $1.2bn of crypto stolen from Binance, part of $2.7bn frozen as recovery efforts continue.

The Onchain Brief EditorsNov 19, 2026 · 20:27 UTC2 min read

The US Justice Department says it has court approval to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance. The department announced the order on Nov. 12, saying it covers part of the funds frozen since the theft.

In its announcement of the Binance asset action, the department said government agencies and private companies had frozen more than $2.7 billion in stolen assets so far. The $1.2 billion covered by the court authorization is part of that frozen total.

What does the court authorization mean?

It allows the US to seize or restrain the assets, but it does not settle who will ultimately receive them. The Associated Press report on the seizure said a judge would determine who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds in a forfeiture case.

The Justice Department said efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the remaining stolen cryptocurrency are continuing. It said the actors have moved funds through crypto bridges, mixers and exchanges. Bridges move assets between crypto networks; mixers can obscure where funds came from.

How much was stolen from Binance?

The department said the theft happened on or about Oct. 29 and involved more than $6 billion in virtual assets. The AP reported the loss at about $6.3 billion and described it as the largest crypto theft on record.

The FBI refers to the North Korean military actors it blames as TraderTraitor, according to the Justice Department. The department said US agencies are working with virtual asset service providers, companies that handle crypto transactions, to track and block the stolen funds.

How much could still be recovered?

The public figures describe different stages of the recovery effort. More than $2.7 billion has been frozen by government and private sector efforts, while the court has authorized seizure or restraint of more than $1.2 billion of that amount.

The Justice Department has not said that the full $1.2 billion has been forfeited or returned to Binance. It said recovery work is ongoing, as the suspected hackers continue trying to move the assets. The court process will determine who can claim funds that are ultimately recovered.
