EU proposal targets crypto networks aiding Russia

EU’s proposed 22nd Russia sanctions package would target crypto and payment networks, including in third countries helping Moscow evade existing restrictions.

The Onchain Brief EditorsNov 27, 2026 · 10:44 UTC2 min read

The European Commission has proposed targeting crypto and payment networks in a new round of sanctions on Russia, including platforms in third countries accused of helping Moscow evade existing restrictions. The proposal is part of the EU’s 22nd sanctions package, presented on Nov. 23, according to the Associated Press report.

Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said the package would focus on Russia and countries that help it get around EU sanctions. She also said the Commission had launched a Crypto Sanctions Task Force to support the work and coordinate with international partners. The statement did not name crypto platforms or spell out the specific restrictions proposed for them.

The financial measures would extend a transaction ban to about 60 Russian banks and remove them from SWIFT, the messaging system banks use to communicate about international payments. The Commission’s statement said it was also targeting crypto and payment networks, but gave no details on which networks could be affected or how the restrictions would work.

The proposal also covers energy and shipping. The Commission wants to bar European companies from helping build, operate or repair Russian energy infrastructure, including refineries. It said the proposed energy services ban would, for the first time, cover electricity services. More than 70 additional ships would be listed, and the Commission wants to ban tankers insured in Russia from EU ports.

Other measures would bar Russian teams and athletes from competing under Russia’s flag at sports events in the EU, ban official Russian symbols at events, and restrict money from Russian sponsors to European sports and cultural groups. Von der Leyen also proposed a visa ban for Russian ex-combatants and restrictions on Europeans holding governing posts at companies in Russia, as well as on Russians holding posts in European sports federations. These details are set out in the European Commission president’s statement.

The measures are proposals, not rules already in force. All 27 EU member countries must approve them before they take effect. The AP reported that the approval process can take weeks and the measures may change during negotiations. The Commission did not give a date for a decision or identify which crypto services could face restrictions.
