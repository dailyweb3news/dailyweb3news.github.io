Bitcoin tops $80,000 as Zcash gains nearly 12%

Bitcoin traded near $80,484 and Zcash gained 11.7% as crypto prices rebounded after Democrats won both houses, while traders weighed policy risks.

The Onchain Brief EditorsNov 5, 2026 · 23:56 UTC2 min read

Bitcoin held above $80,000 and Zcash climbed 11.7% on Thursday as crypto prices rebounded after Democrats won both houses of the U.S. Congress, according to CoinDesk’s market report. The gains followed an initial selloff as election results came in.

How far did Bitcoin and Zcash rise?

Bitcoin traded around $80,484 during Asian morning hours, up 5.7% over 24 hours, CoinDesk reported, citing CoinGecko data. Ether rose 8% to about $2,425. Zcash reached about $1,318, with $1.2 billion traded over the previous day. Zcash is a cryptocurrency whose network can hide the sender, recipient and amount in encrypted payments.

Bitcoin had briefly fallen toward $75,700 early Wednesday before buyers returned. Bloomberg News reported that it later rose as much as 4.2% to just above $81,000, recovering alongside stocks. Those price snapshots were taken at different times.

What helped the market recover?

Crypto prices rose alongside a wider market rebound. BTSE exchange chief operating officer Jeff Mei told CoinDesk that investors appeared to expect the Iran conflict and high oil prices to ease. Brent crude fell more than 8% on Wednesday, U.S. stocks rallied and the dollar weakened, the report said.

The price move came after Democrats won control of Congress, raising questions about the outlook for U.S. crypto rules. Mei told CoinDesk that the CLARITY Act, a bill that would set rules for U.S. cryptocurrency markets, was unlikely to pass in its current form. The election’s effect on future legislation remains uncertain.

What is behind Zcash’s gains?

Zcash’s rise came ahead of NU7, a planned network upgrade expected around Friday. CoinDesk reported that the upgrade would cut the target time between blocks—from 75 seconds to 25. Blocks are batches of transactions added to the network. If an exchange kept its current requirement of three confirmations, the expected wait for a deposit could fall from nearly four minutes to about 75 seconds, the report said.

The election may also bring more scrutiny of financial privacy and illicit finance, according to a policy memo posted on the Zcash forum and cited by CoinDesk. The memo argued for private payments on consumer safety, business confidentiality and civil liberties grounds. It does not establish how the incoming Congress will act.
