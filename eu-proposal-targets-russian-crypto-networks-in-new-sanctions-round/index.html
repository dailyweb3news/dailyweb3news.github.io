EU proposal targets Russian crypto networks in new sanctions round

The EU has proposed targeting Russian crypto and payment networks in a new sanctions package, but all 27 member states must approve it before it takes effect.

The Onchain Brief EditorsNov 28, 2026 · 07:52 UTC2 min read

The European Commission has proposed targeting Russian crypto and payment networks in a new round of sanctions, alongside measures against banks and Russian participation in European sports. The proposal was announced on Nov. 23 as the EU’s 22nd sanctions package against Russia over its war in Ukraine.

The Associated Press report on the proposal says the package would extend transaction bans to 60 Russian banks and remove them from SWIFT, the messaging system banks use for international payments. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said the crypto measures could also cover countries helping Russia evade sanctions.

In her statement announcing the package, von der Leyen said the Commission had launched a Crypto Sanctions Task Force to support the work. The proposal targets crypto and payment networks, but the Commission has not publicly specified which platforms or services would be affected.

The Commission also proposed adding more than 70 ships to its sanctions list and banning tanker vessels insured in Russia from EU ports. Its statement says European companies would be barred from helping Russia build, operate or repair energy infrastructure, including refineries.

On sports, the proposal would bar Russian teams and athletes from competing under the Russian flag at events in the EU. It would also prohibit official Russian symbols at those events and restrict Russian sponsors’ financial flows to European sports and cultural groups, according to the Commission.

The package is still a proposal. All 27 EU member states must endorse it before it can take effect, the AP reported. That process can take weeks, and the measures may change during negotiations. The proposal’s announcement does not mean crypto services are already subject to new restrictions.

The Commission says its aim is to make it harder for Russia to finance the war and to close routes used to evade existing sanctions. The final text, including which crypto networks or third-country services could be covered, will depend on what EU governments approve.
