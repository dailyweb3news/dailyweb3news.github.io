Griffin raises $20 million for stablecoin and card services

Griffin raised $20 million in a Series B led by Octagon Partners to add card issuing and stablecoin reserve and cross-border payment services to its UK banking platform.

The Onchain Brief EditorsNov 13, 2026 · 12:17 UTC2 min read

UK bank Griffin has raised $20 million in a Series B round to expand into card issuing and stablecoin services. The funding will support reserve services for stablecoin businesses and cross-border payments that run on a blockchain, according to Griffin’s funding announcement.

Octagon Partners led the round. Notion Capital, A+ Ventures, Nordic Ninja and Breega also took part, alongside other existing investors. Griffin says the investment values the company at more than $100 million. It did not give an exact valuation.

Stablecoin services are still in development

A stablecoin is a digital token designed to keep a steady value, often by tracking a currency. Griffin plans to develop reserve capabilities for businesses operating in the stablecoin market and add on-chain cross-border payments to its banking platform.

That could let fintech companies use conventional banking and blockchain payment services through one provider. Griffin says some of its existing customers already use stablecoins for cross-border payments. The company has not named the stablecoins or blockchains it plans to support, or given a launch date. The services are planned additions, not products the announcement says are already live.

The round will also fund card issuing. Griffin says its customers will be able to offer branded debit cards through its infrastructure, with a credit card product planned later. The funding supports both product lines and, according to Griffin, is intended to carry the company through to profitability.

Griffin reports growth across its platform

Griffin says more than 80 companies use its banking infrastructure, including Uber, Countingup, Anna Money, Yonder and Prosper. The platform reaches more than 300,000 retail and business customers and processes nearly 10 million payments a year, according to the company’s announcement.

For the year ending September 2026, Griffin reported recurring revenue growth of 3.2 times year on year. The company says that figure is based on unaudited accounts and may change after its statutory audit. It did not disclose its current recurring revenue.

Griffin received a full UK banking licence in 2024, according to CoinDesk’s report on the funding. The latest round gives it capital to build the new services, but the announcement does not say when customers will be able to use them.
