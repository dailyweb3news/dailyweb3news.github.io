What a Fraxswap LP token really represents

A Fraxswap LP token represents a share of a pool’s changing reserves, not a fixed amount of either token. Here is how deposits and withdrawals work.

The Onchain Brief EditorsNov 6, 2026 · 11:29 UTC3 min read

A fraxswap LP token represents a share of a pool’s current reserves: the two tokens held for trading. It does not promise a fixed amount of either one. The share changes in value and composition as trades move the reserves and fees may accumulate.

Fraxswap pairs two assets in a pool, and its core market maker follows the same basic design as Uniswap V2. The fraxswap.co reference is useful when identifying the service, but the claim itself comes from the pool contract: LP tokens track a holder’s portion of its issued shares. Think of them as a receipt for a fraction of the pool, not a separate stash of coins.

How does a Fraxswap LP token map to pool assets?

Your share is your LP token balance divided by the pool’s total LP token supply. If you hold one tenth of all the LP tokens, you have a claim to one tenth of each reserve when you withdraw. For example, if a pool has 1,000 units of one asset and 500 of the other, a 10% share corresponds to 100 and 50 units at that moment.

The pool mints tokens when liquidity is added and burns them when liquidity is removed. The actual amounts returned are calculated from the reserves and total supply at withdrawal. A wallet balance alone does not show the current claim; you also need the pool’s balances and total LP supply.

Why can the claim change after I deposit?

Swaps change the reserves. If traders buy one asset from the pool, less of it remains, while more of the other asset comes in. Fraxswap also supports long-term trades that execute over time. These trades affect the pool as they run, so the assets available to LPs can shift even without a single large swap.

Trading fees may remain in the pool and increase the reserves represented by each LP token. But the two assets’ market prices can move at different rates. That can leave an LP with a different mix, and sometimes a lower total value, than simply holding the original tokens. This difference is often called impermanent loss; the name does not guarantee that the gap will later disappear.

What should I check before withdrawing?

Check the specific pair, its current reserves, and your share of total LP supply. The share tells you the proportion; the reserves tell you what that proportion currently means. A simple checklist helps:

  • Confirm the pool contains the two token contracts you intended.
  • Compare your LP balance with the pool’s total supply.
  • Look at both reserve amounts, not only their combined dollar value.
  • Remember that the amounts can change before a withdrawal transaction completes.

Fraxswap LP tokens are transferable, so a token holder can pass that pool share to another address. Whoever holds the tokens can use them to withdraw the corresponding portion under the pool’s rules. The useful takeaway is simple: read an LP token as a changing percentage claim on a specific pool, not as a fixed deposit or a guaranteed return.
