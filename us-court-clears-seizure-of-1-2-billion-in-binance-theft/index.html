US court clears seizure of $1.2 billion in Binance theft

US authorities have court approval to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of Binance funds, part of $2.7 billion frozen after an October theft.

The Onchain Brief EditorsNov 19, 2026 · 08:18 UTC2 min read

US authorities have court approval to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, the Justice Department said Thursday. The amount is part of more than $2.7 billion in stolen assets that the US government and private companies have frozen so far, according to the Justice Department announcement.

The $1.2 billion is part of a larger freeze

The court authorization does not mean the government has already taken final ownership of the funds. The department said it had obtained permission to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion from the pool of frozen assets. A restraint can prevent funds from moving while legal steps continue.

In such cases, the government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who decides who has a valid claim to the recovered assets, the Associated Press reported. The Justice Department said work to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the stolen crypto is still under way.

FBI links the theft to North Korean hackers

The theft happened on or around Oct. 29. The department said members of the North Korean military, whom the FBI calls TraderTraitor, stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance. The FBI had previously attributed the attack to the group, according to the Associated Press report.

US law enforcement agencies worked with virtual asset service providers, companies that hold or transfer crypto, to stop the stolen assets from moving. The Justice Department said TraderTraitor actors continue to launder funds through crypto bridges, mixers and exchanges. Bridges move assets between blockchains; mixers obscure where crypto came from.

Recovery efforts continue

The government has not said how much of the frozen crypto will ultimately be forfeited or returned, or when that process may finish. The court authorization covers more than $1.2 billion of the frozen total, while the broader effort to track and recover the assets remains active.

The Justice Department also described a separate North Korean-linked scheme called FaceStealer. It said that operation stole more than $400 million in cryptocurrency over the previous 18 months. The department presented the Binance recovery action and the FaceStealer disruption in the same announcement, but said they were separate efforts.
